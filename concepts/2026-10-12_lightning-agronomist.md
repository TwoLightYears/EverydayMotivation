# The Sky's Fertilizer Scientist

**Pairing:** Lightning Bolt as Agronomist

## The hook
Each lightning stroke heats a channel of air to roughly 30,000 K — hotter than the surface of the Sun — forcing atmospheric N₂ and O₂ through the Zel'dovich mechanism into nitric oxide; globally, this cracked nitrogen oxidises and rains down as nitrate at a rate of about **8 Tg N per year**, a free, plant-usable fertiliser falling on fields and forests (see e.g. Levy et al., *JGR* 1996; Schumann & Huntrieser, *ACP* 2007).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette** (drawn from the real coloration of a lightning strike and the field it feeds):
  - `#0A0D1E` — storm indigo (night sky, substrate)
  - `#2B2F55` — bruised cloud underside / mid-tone
  - `#E8EEFF` — plasma-channel white-blue (the bolt core)
  - `#F4D35E` — hot-yellow lightning heart / grain tone
  - `#7FB069` — chlorophyll green (crop / growing nitrate absorber)
  - `#8893A8` — cool draftsman gray (grid, type secondary)
- **Mood:** charged, exacting, scientific-illustrative
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A single, hard-edged lightning bolt rakes down from upper-right through the center of a cross-sectional "chemistry plate." Along its channel, three molecular reaction snapshots are tacked: `N≡N`, `O=O`, and the formed `N–O` — each in a small annotated callout like a scientist's margin note.
  - Below the horizon: a schematic soil cross-section with a single stylised wheat stalk whose roots glow faintly green, catching nitrate droplets (`NO₃⁻`) that trickle down from the bolt's lower length.
  - A measurement scale on the right edge marks altitude; a chemistry-plate frame (crop marks, a small `≈ 30,000 K` tag) says this is a lab plate, not a weather photo.
  - Strong type block in the lower-left: display italic title, uppercase ROLE tag, single-line hook.
- **Motion:** one primary easing-driven motion — the bolt flashes into place with a spring, holds; along its path the reaction callouts appear in sequence (N₂ → •N + •N, O₂ → •O + •O, then NO precipitates); nitrate droplets then drip from the bolt's base to the roots, which gently pulse green. A 5-second loop.
