# The Insect That Reinvented the Pulsejet

**Pairing:** Bombardier Beetle (*Stenaptinus insignis*) as Aerospace Engineer

## The hook

Inside its abdomen the bombardier beetle carries a two-chamber combustion reactor: hydroquinone and hydrogen peroxide are stored in a reservoir, then flushed into a reaction chamber whose walls are coated with catalase and peroxidase; the exothermic decomposition flashes to ~100 °C and ejects a boiling defensive spray in discrete micropulses at roughly 500 Hz — a pulsejet mechanism functionally analogous to the V-1 engine, and one that aerospace researchers have modelled to improve real combustion-chamber design (Eisner & Aneshansley, *PNAS* 1999; Beheshti & McIntosh, *Bioinspiration & Biomimetics* 2007).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 portrait
- **Palette:** drawn from *Stenaptinus insignis* coloration and heat.
  - `#0E1119` — deep ink (background board)
  - `#F5EBD3` — warm cream (paper, text)
  - `#D9531F` — rust orange (thorax, legs, warning)
  - `#F5A83A` — warm yellow (heat, spray glow)
  - `#2E3B54` — iridescent dark blue (elytra shadow)
- **Mood:** studious, incandescent, exacting.
- **Key visual elements:**
  1. A precise side-profile of the beetle occupying the upper 60% of the frame, drawn as a scientific cutaway that exposes the two-chamber reactor inside its abdomen.
  2. Signature "prop" of the aerospace-engineer role: a labelled pulsejet diagram — reservoir, reaction chamber, nozzle — overlaid on the anatomy so the biology reads as a schematic.
  3. A staccato arc of ejected pulses trailing off the nozzle into the right margin, each dot smaller than the last, dramatizing the ~500 Hz firing rate.
- **Motion:** one primary spring-driven entrance for the beetle and its schematic; a subtle, easing-driven pulse train that fires micropulses off the nozzle in staccato bursts over a ~6 s loop. Everything else stays still, so the eye reads the firing as the argument.
