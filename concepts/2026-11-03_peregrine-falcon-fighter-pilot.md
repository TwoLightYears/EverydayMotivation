# The 390-km/h Test Pilot

**Pairing:** Peregrine Falcon (*Falco peregrinus*) as Fighter Pilot

## The hook
In a hunting stoop the peregrine folds into a teardrop and accelerates past 320 km/h (389 km/h measured in Ken Franklin's instrumented 2005 skydiving stoops), and tiny bony cones in each nostril — the *nasal tubercles* — split the oncoming airstream so the bird can keep breathing against ~0.1 atm of ram pressure; the same inlet-cone geometry is standard on supersonic jet engines.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0A0E16` — midnight ink (sky / negative space)
  - `#2E3A4A` — slate blue-gray (peregrine mantle / back plumage)
  - `#D4C3A2` — buff cream (breast feather base)
  - `#8B4A2B` — rust / sepia (chestnut barring, accent, needle)
  - `#C8D7E8` — pale sky (fine detail, grid lines, altitude ticks)
- **Mood:** aerodynamic, exacting, cold-air stillness
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A large circular airspeed-indicator gauge centred top-two-thirds — hash marks, major ticks at 0 / 100 / 200 / 300 / 400 km/h, a rust-coloured needle that sweeps toward 390.
  - A stylised peregrine silhouette in a stoop (teardrop body, wings tight to flanks) diving *through* the gauge, with thin wind streaks curving around it.
  - A small circled technical inset in the lower-right of the drafting frame: `FIG. 2 · NASAL TUBERCLE`, showing the bony cone splitting an incoming air arrow.
  - A strong display-type lockup under the frame: role tag `FIGHTER PILOT`, large italic Playfair title, hook line in Inter.
- **Motion:** the airspeed needle springs from 0 to ~390 km/h on a damped easing while the falcon silhouette descends from the top of the gauge toward its centre; wind streaks lengthen as the needle climbs. A single ~4.5 s hero loop.
