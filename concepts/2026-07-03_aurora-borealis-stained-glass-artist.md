# The Sky's Stained-Glass Artist

**Pairing:** Aurora Borealis as a Stained-Glass Artist

## The hook

Every colour in the aurora is a labelled pane of atmosphere: atomic oxygen glows green at **557.7 nm** between roughly **100–300 km**, red at **630.0 nm** above ~200 km (where collisions are rare enough for the metastable O(¹D) state to radiate), and ionised molecular nitrogen N₂⁺ burns blue-violet at **427.8 nm** below ~100 km. Read the sky like glass — the hue tells you which gas, at which altitude, is emitting.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait).
- **Palette (drawn from real auroral emission lines):**
  - `#050820` — deep polar-night background
  - `#6E4CFF` — 427.8 nm nitrogen violet (low altitude)
  - `#7CFF9A` — 557.7 nm oxygen green (mid altitude)
  - `#FF3D5A` — 630.0 nm oxygen crimson (high altitude)
  - `#EFE5FF` — lead-came / starlight highlight
- **Mood:** reverent, luminous, precise (cathedral hush).
- **Key visual elements:**
  - **Focal object:** a Gothic lancet stained-glass window occupying the top three-quarters of the frame, its glass painted with vertical auroral curtains.
  - **Role's signature prop:** heavy dark lead came — outer frame, central mullion, horizontal transoms — dividing the window into three altitude panes.
  - **The fusion idea:** each pane is *simultaneously* a stained-glass compartment and an altitude band; the wavelength and gas species are inscribed inside like a maker's mark, so the window functions as an atmospheric spectrum.
- **Motion:** the panes light up from the bottom (violet, low altitude) to the top (crimson, high altitude) on a staggered spring, then breathe as slow auroral curtains drift laterally — a single primary motion that dramatises how altitude → gas → colour.
