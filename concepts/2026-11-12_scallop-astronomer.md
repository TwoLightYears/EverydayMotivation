# The Mirror-Eyed Astronomer

**Pairing:** Scallop (*Pecten maximus*) as Observatory Astronomer

## The hook
A single scallop sees the world through as many as 200 bright-blue eyes ringing its mantle, and each eye forms its image not with a lens but with a concave **mirror** built from a mosaic of squared guanine crystals — the same optical principle as a reflecting telescope (Palmer et al., *Science* 358 (2017): 1172–1175).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#070B18` — deep night (background / sea-floor void)
  - `#E8D4A2` — scallop shell cream (shell plate, type secondary)
  - `#C77A4E` — warm shell orange-brown (ribs, mantle, accents)
  - `#4DB3F5` — scallop-eye blue (the real, luminous hue; focal accent)
  - `#8EB9D0` — pale slate / instrument gray (schematic lines, labels)
- **Mood:** nocturnal, instrumental, quietly uncanny
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A top-down half-scallop shell rendered as a technical plate — ribbed, cream over warm orange — with its mantle edge studded by a ring of small glowing blue eyes positioned like observatory domes along a coastline.
  - A labelled cross-section of one eye off to the side: incoming starlight → double retina → concave guanine mirror → return focus, with ray paths drawn in the same instrument-gray as an optics textbook.
  - A faint celestial coordinate grid across the plate, treating the shell as a chart.
  - Strong lower-left type block: display title, uppercase ROLE tag, single-line hook.
- **Motion:** the 200 eyes light up sequentially around the mantle edge on a spring easing (like dome lamps powering on at dusk); a parallel bundle of starlight rays traces through the cross-section, reflects off the parabolic mirror, and converges on the retina; a slow ambient drift on the background star field. 5-second loop.
