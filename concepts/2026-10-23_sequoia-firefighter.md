# The Tree That Waits for Fire

**Pairing:** Giant Sequoia (*Sequoiadendron giganteum*) as Firefighter

## The hook
A mature *Sequoiadendron giganteum* carries bark up to ~60 cm thick that holds almost no flammable resin and is rich in tannins — and its serotinous cones can stay sealed on the tree for up to ~20 years, opening only when the convective heat of a surface fire dries the scales enough to release the seeds inside (Hartesveldt & Harvey, 1967; Harvey, Shellhammer & Stecker, USFS PSW-GTR-44, 1980; US National Park Service).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait)
- **Palette** (drawn from real sequoia bark, fire, and ash):
  - `#0E0905` — char black (background / substrate)
  - `#5B2612` — russet bark shadow
  - `#B35C2C` — sun-warmed bark amber
  - `#F0B04A` — ember / fire glow
  - `#3F4F23` — needle sage (secondary accent)
  - `#E8DAB8` — ash cream (type)
- **Mood:** patient, elemental, warm-cold tension (fire against still forest)
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A tall, stylised sequoia trunk running the full height of the right side of the frame — vertical bark furrows in russet and amber, scaled against a 60 cm bark-thickness callout.
  - A low ember-glow at the base of the trunk with a single serotinous cone cracked open above it, seeds drifting down from the opened scales.
  - Left half: generous negative space holding the type lockup (role tag, big italic display title, single-line hook, footer citation) aligned to a strict margin grid.
  - Thin technical callouts (bark depth, cone age, seed count) in draftsman gray-cream — the "firefighter's" field notes.
- **Motion:** one primary motion — a slow upward glow-wave that travels the trunk once per loop (heat rising through the bark layers), while embers at the base pulse on a spring and one cone scale eases open with a short cluster of seeds falling. 5-second loop; spring/cubic easing throughout, no linear ramps.
