# The Falcon at Mach 0.32

**Pairing:** Peregrine Falcon (*Falco peregrinus*) as Aerospace Test Pilot

## The hook
Stereo-videography of wild peregrines in a hunting stoop clocked terminal dive speeds up to 89 m/s — roughly 320 km/h, Mach 0.32 — while a translucent nictitating third eyelid sweeps the cornea and small bony tubercles inside each nostril throttle the incoming air, an anatomical inlet cone that inspired analogies to the moveable intake spikes of supersonic jets (Ponitz et al., *PLOS ONE* 9(2): e86506, 2014).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0F1622` — cold sky-ink (deep hood / negative space)
  - `#43587A` — slate blue-gray (falcon back, plumage)
  - `#E7DFCB` — cream (falcon underside, primary type)
  - `#EDC341` — cere yellow (accent, needles, cues)
  - `#8B3E33` — auburn brown (bar accent, danger tick)
- **Mood:** cold, taut, aeronautical
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A stylised falcon silhouette in a full teardrop tuck, angled steeply down-right, held motionless while streamlines of air rip past it.
  - A cockpit-instrument grammar: analog airspeed dial reading in KM/H, altimeter tape ticking down the left rail, thin callout leaders labelling the anatomical inlet spike and the nictitating membrane.
  - Strong lower-left type block: uppercase ROLE tag, italic display title, single-line hook.
- **Motion:** streamlines flow past the falcon (world moves, bird holds); the airspeed needle sweeps from 0 up to 320 KM/H on a spring; the altimeter tape scrolls; callouts and title snap in on ease-out. A 5-second still-hero loop.
