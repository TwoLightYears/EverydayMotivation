# The stone floor that laid itself.

**Pairing:** Basalt Columns (columnar basalt, exemplified by Antrim's Giant's Causeway) as **Master Tile Setter**

## The hook

When a thick basalt lava flow cools, it contracts and cracks so that fractures propagate along paths minimising the elastic strain energy released per unit new crack area; the network converges on 120° triple‑junctions — the tightest planar packing — producing a near‑hexagonal tessellation. At the Giant's Causeway roughly 40,000 prisms result, dominated by 6‑sided columns with 5‑ and 7‑sided ones filling the statistical tail (Goehring, Mahadevan & Morris, *PNAS* 2009; Aydin & DeGraff, *Science* 1988).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, 4:5)
- **Palette (from real basalt/Antrim coloration):**
  - `#0F1218` — near‑black basalt (background ink)
  - `#242932` — wet stone / column tops
  - `#6A7580` — stone highlight rim
  - `#B15A2A` — iron‑oxide rust bloom
  - `#C6BE9C` — pale lichen / bone
- **Mood:** austere, geometric, timeless — a craftsman's technical plate.
- **Key visual elements:**
  - Focal object: an overhead field of hexagonal basalt column tops, tessellating outward from a seed with irregular 5‑ / 6‑ / 7‑sided cells (as at the Causeway), each column slightly recessed with a rimlight to read as solid stone.
  - Signature prop: a tile setter's plumb bob (with cord) draped across the hex floor and a small trowel silhouette, drawn as thin technical linework — the setter's tools sitting idle atop stone that has already been laid.
  - Composition fusion: one hex is annotated as a specimen — 120° internal‑angle callouts on three vertices, a "STRAIN‑ENERGY MIN" tag — treating the natural fracture pattern as a completed shop drawing.
- **Motion:** crystallisation sweep — hexagons fade in with a spring‑eased radial cascade from the seed cell outward, as though the fracture network is propagating; the callouts and tools resolve last. Calm 6‑second loop.
