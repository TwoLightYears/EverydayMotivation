# The keeper of every year

**Pairing:** Bristlecone Pine (*Pinus longaeva*) as Time-Capsule Archivist

## The hook

In California's White Mountains stands **Methuselah**, a Great Basin bristlecone pine that Edmund Schulman ring-dated in 1957 at 4,789 years old — meaning it has now recorded at least **4,855 unbroken annual growth rings**, and was already a mature tree when the pyramids at Giza were being cut. Each ring is a dated environmental ledger: its width tracks that year's precipitation and its ¹⁴C content preserves that year's solar activity, so bristlecone chronologies now anchor the IntCal radiocarbon calibration curve back to ~8000 BCE (Reimer et al., IntCal20, *Radiocarbon* 62, 2020).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:** drawn from real bristlecone coloration — weathered, high-desert wood.
  - `#161311` — cold-desert ink (background)
  - `#E8E1D3` — sun-bleached weathered wood (highest-contrast rings)
  - `#D9C7A0` — warm cream heartwood
  - `#B58447` — amber sap / resinous glow
  - `#4B3A28` — deep bark shadow / cracks
- **Mood:** patient, exacting, geologic
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A large off-centre cross-section of tree rings anchored to the upper-right of a manuscript-like grid — the archivist's ledger. Ring widths vary annually (drought years thin, wet years thick).
  - Four leader-line callouts from specific rings to historic events dated to those years (Great Pyramid ~2560 BCE, Stonehenge trilithons ~2500 BCE, Buddha born ~563 BCE, printing press 1440 CE).
  - Bottom-left type lockup: uppercase ROLE tag, italic display title, hook body.
  - Small marginalia: pith cross ("×") at the tree's centre, a compass-style north indicator, and a Ferguson-chronology caption strip.
- **Motion:** the primary easing-driven motion is **radial accretion** — rings appear one after another from the pith outward on a decelerating spring, so the viewer literally watches the archive being written. The four leader-line callouts fade in as their ring is deposited. A single hero still (with a 5-second growth loop for video) both work.
