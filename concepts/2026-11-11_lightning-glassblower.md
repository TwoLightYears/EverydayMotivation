# The Sky's Glassblower

**Pairing:** Lightning as Glassblower

## The hook
When a cloud-to-ground lightning strike enters sand, its plasma channel briefly reaches ~30,000 K — about five times the surface of the Sun — and vitrifies silica into a hollow, branching glass tube known as a fulgurite, with a vitreous lining of lechatelierite (amorphous SiO₂) that traces the exact path of the current through the soil. It is a complete glass object, cast by the sky in milliseconds.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait)
- **Palette (hex, real coloration of lightning + fulgurite + sand):**
  - `#FFF3B0` — lightning plasma core (near-white yellow)
  - `#F8B64C` — hot molten silica / fulgurite glow
  - `#C9A26B` — surface sand
  - `#2B1C3D` — stormy sky / deep indigo
  - `#0E0A18` — subsoil / near-black
- **Mood:** primordial, electric, suspended
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A single stylised lightning bolt descending from a dark sky, striking sand at the horizon line
  - Below ground — rendered as a geological cross-section — the bolt continues as a branching, backlit fulgurite tube (the role's "work": a glass sculpture cast in the dark)
  - The glassblower's signature prop reinterpreted: the bolt itself is the blowpipe; the earth is the furnace
- **Motion:** The bolt strikes top-to-ground on a fast spring (≈ 0.3 s). On impact, the fulgurite then draws itself branch-by-branch downward through the sand on a cubic ease-out (≈ 1.6 s), followed by a slow warm pulse along the glass walls. Calm, intentional, 5 s loop.
