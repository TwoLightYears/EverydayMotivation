# The Pulsed-Jet Chemist

**Pairing:** Bombardier Beetle (*Brachinus* / *Stenaptinus insignis*) as Chemical Engineer

## The hook
Inside its abdomen, the bombardier beetle keeps hydroquinones and ~25% hydrogen peroxide in a storage reservoir; when threatened it mixes them in a thick-walled reaction chamber lined with catalases and peroxidases, driving an exothermic oxidation that heats the mixture to ~100 °C and ejects it as a steaming benzoquinone spray — pulsed at **368–735 Hz** and aimed through a swivelling nozzle, as imaged directly by Arndt et al. via synchrotron X-ray in *Science* **348**, 428–431 (2015).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:** (drawn from the beetle's real coloration)
  - `#0B0E13` — abyssal ink (background / negative space)
  - `#C24324` — carapace vermillion (Brachinus pronotum/legs)
  - `#F6A528` — reactor amber (hot pulse / caution yellow)
  - `#F2E8D4` — vapor cream (quinone steam trail)
  - `#7B8A99` — draftsman slate (schematic grid, secondary type)
- **Mood:** exacting, hazardous, patent-blueprint calm
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A patent-style cross-section schematic of the beetle in profile, abdomen cut away to reveal two labelled reservoirs feeding a central reaction chamber, with a swivelling nozzle at the posterior.
  - Numbered callouts (1–5) in a chemistry-textbook grammar: reservoir → valve → catalyst chamber → nozzle → pulsed ejection.
  - A pulsed amber jet arcing out of the nozzle in concentric wavefronts, with a small HUD reading `100 °C · ~500 Hz`.
  - Faint drafting grid underneath — the engineer's drafting board.
- **Motion:** two propellants charge the reservoirs → a ring-pulse travels through the mixing valve → the chamber flashes amber → a high-frequency pulse train fires from the nozzle at ~500 Hz, each puff cooling to cream vapor downstream. Loops over ~5 seconds.
