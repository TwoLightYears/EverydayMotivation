# The Two-Factor Bouncer

**Pairing:** Venus Flytrap (*Dionaea muscipula*) as Two-Factor Bouncer

## The hook

A Venus flytrap will not close on the first touch of its trigger hairs; a second touch must arrive within roughly 20 seconds, because each mechanical stimulus injects a transient pulse of cytosolic Ca²⁺ that decays over time — closure fires only when the second pulse pushes the concentration past threshold (Böhm et al., *Current Biology* 26 (2016) 286–295). The trap is running two-factor authentication in wet chemistry, and the timeout is the second-long half-life of a calcium signal.

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette** — drawn from the trap's real coloration:

  - `#1E4D34` — outer leaf green
  - `#8CB84A` — lime rim / trigger-hair highlight
  - `#B4213A` — trap-interior crimson (anthocyanin)
  - `#F1E8D2` — cream cilia / type highlight
- **Mood:** botanical, forensic, quietly ticking
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A single Venus flytrap trap seen in three-quarter cross-section, filling the top two-thirds — inner face crimson, outer margin dark green, six trigger hairs standing on the two lobes.
  - Two touch events rendered as pulse rings on two of the hairs, labelled `TOUCH 1` and `TOUCH 2`, connected by a running 20-second timeline gauge — the "bouncer's clock".
  - A blueprint-style Ca²⁺ decay curve tucked into the lower composition, marking the threshold that closure crosses on the second pulse.
  - Editorial masthead top; role tag + display headline + one-line hook lower-left, aligned to a strict 80 px margin grid.
- **Motion:** the first touch pulses on frame ~30 and adds a small step to the Ca²⁺ curve that then decays; the second touch pulses on frame ~90 and pushes the curve above the dashed threshold; on that crossing the trap snaps shut with a fast spring (≈100 ms), the trigger hairs vanishing inside a green seal — one dramatic beat, then hold.
