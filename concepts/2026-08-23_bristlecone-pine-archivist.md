# The Living Archive

**Pairing:** Bristlecone Pine (*Pinus longaeva*) as Archivist

## The hook
A single Great Basin bristlecone in California's White Mountains — the tree known as Methuselah — has laid down ~4,857 annual growth rings, and by cross-matching that living wood to dead wood on the same slope, dendrochronologists built an unbroken 8,800-year ring record whose narrow, frost-scarred rings anchor the IntCal calibration of radiocarbon dating and even preserve the climate fingerprint of the 43 BC Okmok II eruption.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0B1220` — pre-dawn desert sky (background / negative space)
  - `#E8DFCF` — sun-bleached bristlecone bone (rings, primary type)
  - `#C69B4A` — warm heartwood amber (accent rings, hairlines)
  - `#7A6D5A` — weathered driftwood shadow (secondary type, grid)
  - `#2E4C4B` — sagebrush teal (frost-ring markers)
- **Mood:** patient, exacting, archival — a card-catalog quiet.
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A large cross-section of tree rings positioned as the focal object — offset lower-right so the type block breathes in the upper-left. Rings vary in width to reflect real climate years; a handful of narrow "frost" rings are marked in teal.
  - Fine leader lines pull out from specific rings to small archival index cards labelled with year and event (43 BC · Okmok II · frost ring; AD 536 · dust-veil frost; AD 1600 · Huaynaputina; 1816 · Tambora "year without a summer"; 4855 BC · Methuselah pith).
  - A thin dendrochronologist's ledger grid underlies the composition, and a scale bar reads in years, not kilometres.
- **Motion:** rings emerge from the pith outward on an ease-out cubic like a slow tide, one every few frames; once the disc is complete, a slow radial pulse re-traces from centre to bark, and the index cards drop into place with a spring after the disc settles. A ~5s single-hero motion.
