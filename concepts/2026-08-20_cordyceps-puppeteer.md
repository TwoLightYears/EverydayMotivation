# The Muscle-Fibre Puppeteer

**Pairing:** Ophiocordyceps unilateralis (Zombie-Ant Fungus) as Puppeteer

## The hook
3-D confocal reconstructions of *Ophiocordyceps unilateralis* inside its Camponotus host ants show that the fungus never touches the brain — instead, an interconnected mycelial network occupies up to ~40 % of the ant's body cavity and wraps individual mandibular muscle fibres, physically pulling them shut like strings on a marionette to force the "death-grip" bite onto a leaf vein at ~25 cm above the forest floor, exactly the humidity and temperature the fungus needs to fruit and cast spores (Fredericksen et al., *PNAS* 114:47, 2017; Hughes et al., *BMC Ecology* 11:13, 2011).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 x 1350 (portrait, social plate)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0B0E09` — humid forest-floor ink (substrate / negative space)
  - `#1A2114` — leaf-shadow plate ground
  - `#C89B3C` — Cordyceps stroma ochre (the fruiting body)
  - `#E8D28F` — spore-highlight cream (thin filaments, type accent)
  - `#5A6B2E` — moss-vein green (grid, host chitin secondary)
- **Mood:** eerie, hypnotic, clinical
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A mycological field-plate treatment: a carpenter ant rendered in profile, its mandibles clamped on a horizontal leaf-vein, with a tall Cordyceps stroma erupting from the back of its head.
  - A branching mycelial web threaded through the ant's body, terminating in fine hyphae that wrap the mandibular muscle group (the composition idea that fuses object + role: the fungus IS the puppeteer, and the strings we usually imagine as marionette wires are literally hyphae inside the ant).
  - Overhead, five taut puppeteer strings arc down from the crown of the fungal stroma to the mandibles, front legs, and thorax — the extended phenotype made visible.
  - Formal plate typography: uppercase role tag, italic Playfair title, single-paragraph hook, citation footer, ~25 cm altitude annotation.
- **Motion:** hyphae extend inward through the ant (0–1.6 s) → strings drop from the stroma and go taut with spring easing (1.6–3.0 s) → mandibles clench a final increment onto the vein and a faint spore-pulse breathes across the stroma (3.0–5.0 s). Roughly a 5-second loop.
