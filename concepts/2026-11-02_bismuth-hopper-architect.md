# The Mineral That Builds Its Own Ziggurat

**Pairing:** Bismuth Hopper Crystal as Architect

## The hook
When molten bismuth (element 83, melting point 271.5 °C) cools in open air it grows *hopper* crystals: atoms snap onto edges and corners far faster than onto the flat centres of each face, so every face recedes into a stepped-ziggurat void — a staircase etched inward by its own crystallography. The rainbow surface is not pigment; it is a bismuth-oxide (Bi₂O₃) skin on the order of 100 nm thick, colouring the metal by thin-film interference.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait social poster)
- **Palette** (drawn from real bismuth-oxide interference colours):
  - `#0E1218` — crystal void / background steel
  - `#F3C76A` — oxide gold (first-order interference)
  - `#D94E7A` — oxide magenta
  - `#4FB3C9` — oxide cyan-teal
  - `#6B5CB8` — oxide violet
- **Mood:** angular, iridescent, exacting
- **Key visual elements:**
  - One large isometric bismuth hopper crystal, dead-centre, with each of its three visible faces showing recursive nested terraces receding inward (the hopper void). Each terrace is tinted a different interference colour, so stepping inward also walks the spectrum.
  - A thin "melt line" under the cube with annotation `COOLED FROM MELT · 271 °C`.
  - Thin draftsman annotations in the margins: `ELEVATION / Bi (83)`, `HOPPER GROWTH · EDGE ATTACHMENT ≫ FACE ATTACHMENT` with arrow diagrams, `OXIDE SKIN · Bi₂O₃ ≈ 100 NM · THIN-FILM INTERFERENCE`, a scale bar.
  - Type lockup in the lower third: uppercase `ROLE / ARCHITECT`, large Playfair italic title, and the hook paragraph in Inter.
- **Motion:** faces appear in sequence (top → right → left), and within each face the nested terraces snap inward from outer to centre on a spring (edge-first growth). Once fully built, an iridescent shimmer sweeps upward through the stack. 5-second loop.
