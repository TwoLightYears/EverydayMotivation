# The 30,000-Kelvin Glassblower

**Pairing:** Lightning as Glassblower

## The hook
The channel of a return-stroke lightning bolt reaches roughly **30,000 K within the first ~10 µs — about five times hotter than the Sun's photosphere (~5,778 K)** — and where it enters silica-rich sand it instantly fuses grains along the current path into hollow, branching glass tubes called **fulgurites**; specimens more than four metres long have been logged, each one a solid recording of the strike's exact geometry (Uman, *The Lightning Discharge*, 2001; Pasek et al., *Fulgurite morphology*, 2013).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait poster)
- **Palette** — drawn from the real spectrum of a return-stroke and its fulgurite:
  - `#0A0E1A` — night-sky ink / negative space
  - `#7C6EE8` — violet plasma edge (nitrogen recombination)
  - `#FFFDF4` — bolt hot core
  - `#E6B776` — silica sand
  - `#C88445` — molten fulgurite glass
- **Mood:** electric, awestruck, alchemical.
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A jagged white-hot bolt descending from a slate storm above through the middle of the frame; its edges bleed violet.
  - A precise horizon line at the ground plane; below it, a mirrored subterranean structure of branching hollow glass — the fulgurite — glowing amber at the impact end and cooling into deeper glass toward each root tip. The two halves *rhyme*: the bolt is the pipe, the fulgurite is the piece.
  - Right-hand type stack: uppercase ROLE tag, large italic display title, single-line hook. Draftsman-style tick marks along the horizon; a small temperature callout at the strike point ("≈ 30 000 K").
- **Motion:** one strike per loop. Frame 0: a bright flash + shock ring. Bolt draws downward on a fast ease-in. On contact, the halo blooms and the fulgurite grows *downward* along a spring, branch by branch, cooling from white → amber → dark glass. Between strikes the piece sits still while faint embers pulse. 5-second loop.
