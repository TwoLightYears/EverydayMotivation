# The 224-Hour Athlete

**Pairing:** Bar-tailed Godwit (*Limosa lapponica baueri*) as Ultramarathoner

## The hook
In October 2022 a satellite-tagged juvenile bar-tailed godwit, "B6," flew 13,560 km non-stop from the Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta in Alaska to Ansons Bay, Tasmania in 11 days and 1 hour (~224 h) — no food, no water, no landing — the longest continuous flight ever recorded for any bird (Global Flyway Network; Guinness World Records, 2022; extending the earlier bar-tailed godwit records tracked by Gill et al., *Proc. R. Soc. B* 2009 and Battley et al., *J. Avian Biology* 2012).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 x 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0B1B29` — deep Pacific / night ocean (ground)
  - `#C25A34` — rufous breeding-plumage rust (the bird / accent)
  - `#E9C48A` — buff wing feather / warm mid-tone
  - `#F5EEDF` — cream margin / paper (type)
  - `#6B7B87` — cold spray gray (secondary type, gridlines)
- **Mood:** stoic, oceanic, quietly heroic
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A stylised chart of the Pacific with a **great-circle arc** curving from Alaska (upper-right corner) down to Tasmania (lower-left corner) — the "course."
  - A small, elegant godwit silhouette gliding along the arc, mid-Pacific, alone.
  - Dashed latitude lines and small tick marks along the arc at Day 3 / 6 / 9 / 11 — the split-times, treated like a race clock.
  - A single rust dot at each terminus (start / finish line).
  - Strong lower-left type block: rust ROLE tag, italic display title, one-line hook.
- **Motion:** the arc draws itself in over ~2.6s along a spring easing (start → finish), the godwit rides the tip of the growing line, then settles at the finish; the split-time ticks fade in as the arc passes them. A 5-second loop.
