# The Ledger That Grew

**Pairing:** Great Basin Bristlecone Pine (*Pinus longaeva*) as Archivist

## The hook
A single Great Basin bristlecone pine can live past 4,800 years — the "Methuselah" tree in California's White Mountains was measured at 4,789 rings in 1957 (Schulman, *National Geographic* 1958). Cross-dated bristlecone rings, spliced against sub-fossil trunks, anchor a continuous ~9,000-year dendrochronology that calibrates the radiocarbon time-scale (Ferguson, *Tree-Ring Bulletin* 1969; Reimer et al., IntCal20, *Radiocarbon* 2020) — every ring is a dated, timestamped, tamper-resistant record of temperature, drought, volcanism, and cosmic-ray flux.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#F4EBD3` — aged archival paper (substrate / negative space)
  - `#C69349` — bristlecone bronze (heartwood / primary rings)
  - `#7A3E1F` — resin-dark cambium accent (ink stamps, index arm)
  - `#3B2A1A` — deep charred ink (type primary, deepest rings)
  - `#8A8778` — weathered driftwood gray (grid, secondary type)
- **Mood:** patient, tactile, archival
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A large cross-section of a bristlecone trunk rendered as concentric annotated rings — the tree IS the ledger. A slim radial "index arm" pins specific date events to their rings (536 CE global dimming, 1815 Tambora, 3050 BCE germination, present).
  - A card-catalog block in one margin: SPECIMEN NO. WPN-114 · P. LONGAEVA · WHITE MOUNTAINS · CA.
  - Faint ledger ruling underneath — the archivist's workbook.
  - Type block lower-left: uppercase ROLE tag, italic serif display title, single-line hook.
- **Motion:** rings settle outward from the pith with a spring easing, as if the archivist is drawing them one century at a time; once the outer ring seats, the radial index arm sweeps from 12 o'clock and stops on a flagged event, which pulses once. A 5-second loop.
