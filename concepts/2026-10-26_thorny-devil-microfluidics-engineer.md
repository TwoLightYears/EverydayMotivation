# The lizard that drinks with its skin

**Pairing:** Thorny Devil (*Moloch horridus*) as Microfluidics Engineer

## The hook

Every square millimetre of the Thorny Devil's skin between its spines is etched with semi-tubular capillary channels roughly 50–150 µm wide; these grooves draw water passively against gravity from damp sand up the lizard's legs and flanks to the mouth corners, where rhythmic jaw motions evacuate the fluid — a purely surface-tension-driven microfluidic circuit, no pumping involved (Comanns et al., *Journal of Experimental Biology*, 2017).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080×1350 portrait.
- **Palette:** drawn from the lizard's real desert livery.
  - `#E8B55C` — ochre ridge (sun-side highlight)
  - `#A7591F` — burnt sienna (interstitial shadow)
  - `#F2DEB1` — bone / dry sand (channel highlight)
  - `#1A1308` — deep umber ground (ink)
  - `#6FB7C9` — hygroscopic cyan (water accent, used sparingly)
- **Mood:** parched, exact, elegant.
- **Key visual elements:** a technical plan-view blueprint of the lizard rendered on a drafting board: the body is a vector silhouette lined with thorn marks, and the capillary network traced between them is drawn in cyan as a routed microfluidic circuit converging at the mouth. The role's "signature prop" is a cross-section callout of a single capillary groove with a scale bar in micrometres.
- **Motion:** one primary motion — a cyan water droplet travels along the capillary trace from the hind leg forward to the mouth, with spring easing (slow start, decisive end), while all other elements hold still. A still of the moment the droplet reaches the jaw is the hero frame.
