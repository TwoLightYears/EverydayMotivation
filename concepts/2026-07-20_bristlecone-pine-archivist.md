# The Living Archive

**Pairing:** Great Basin Bristlecone Pine (*Pinus longaeva*) as Archivist

## The hook
Cored by Edmund Schulman in California's White Mountains, the Great Basin bristlecone "Methuselah" has an innermost ring dated to c. 2833 BCE — roughly 4,855 years old — and each of its annual rings preserves a datable record of local climate (ring width, δ¹³C, δ¹⁸O). Cross-dated with dead wood, bristlecones extend an unbroken, year-by-year archive back roughly 9,000 years.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#1A1613` — deep archival ink (substrate / negative space)
  - `#F2E4CE` — parchment / sun-bleached wood highlight
  - `#B0863C` — aged brass label / gilt accent
  - `#7A3A2A` — heartwood iron-oxide red (primary warm)
  - `#8E8676` — weathered silver-gray wood (secondary)
- **Mood:** reverent, precise, weathered
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A large tree-ring cross-section anchored top-right of the drafting field, rendered as a museum specimen with a pith at its centre and dozens of concentric annual rings radiating outward.
  - Thin leader lines pin dated historical events (Great Pyramid, Bronze Age, Common Era, Industrial Revolution, "you are here") to specific rings.
  - A small silhouette of a wind-twisted bristlecone trunk sitting in the left margin like an archivist's stamp.
  - Type block bottom-left: display italic title, brass ROLE tag, single-line hook.
- **Motion:** the rings inscribe outward from the pith one at a time, as though the tree is drawing time itself; once the disc is complete, the leader lines and date tabs settle into place with a spring easing. A ~5-second loop with a slow re-inscription cycle.
