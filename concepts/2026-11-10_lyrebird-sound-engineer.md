# The Bird That Rips Sound

**Pairing:** Superb Lyrebird as Audio Mimicry Engineer

## The hook
The superb lyrebird (*Menura novaehollandiae*) imitates chainsaws, camera shutters, car alarms, and more than twenty other bird species with uncanny fidelity — all with a syrinx that carries only three pairs of intrinsic muscles, *fewer* than the four-plus pairs found in a typical oscine songbird (Ames 1971; Zann & Dunstan 2008).

## Visual brief

**Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 portrait.

**Palette** (drawn from real *M. novaehollandiae* plumage — silvery filamentary tail, rufous-edged lyrates, slate-brown body, forest-floor dusk):
- `#0E100F` — forest dusk (background)
- `#1A1E1C` — board / panel
- `#DDD4BC` — silver tail filament / primary ink
- `#B8692E` — rufous lyrate edge (accent)
- `#8B7F6A` — moss-taupe secondary text / metering

**Mood:** precise, mimetic, hushed-studio.

**Key visual elements:**
- A single tall tail fan rising from bottom-centre, 16 lyrate feathers splayed into an arc.
- The feather shafts double as the channel strips of a recording console — each one a vertical level bar with a numerical read-out and tick marks.
- A thin horizontal waveform threads left→right behind the fan, as if the same signal is being torn apart into frequency bands.
- Small side-profile silhouette of the lyrebird at the base of the fan, beak lifted — the source of the signal.
- Studio-console chrome: engraved scales, a single highlighted channel labelled "SOURCE / CHAINSAW".

**Motion:** one primary spring-driven motion — the fan unfurls from closed to full arc as the 16 EQ bars cascade up to their real levels (left to right), then a thin "playhead" sweeps once across the waveform. Calm, intentional, not busy.
