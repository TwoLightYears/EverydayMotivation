# The 4,800-Year Ledger

**Pairing:** Bristlecone Pine (*Pinus longaeva*) as Archivist

## The hook

In California's White Mountains, individual Great Basin bristlecone pines carry more than 4,800 continuous annual rings — and frost-damaged rings resolve specific volcanic winters (e.g. 1816 CE's Tambora summer and the 536 CE "dust-veil" year) to the exact growing season, giving the chronology single-year precision across the Holocene (Salzer & Hughes, *Quaternary Research* 67, 2007).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait)
- **Palette (drawn from a weathered *P. longaeva* cross-section at tree line):**
  - `#14100C` — night ink, background
  - `#E8DDCA` — bleached sun-silvered wood
  - `#A47142` — ochre inner wood / latewood band
  - `#3C2A1A` — dark fissured heartwood
  - `#D8A24A` — archival gilt (annotation accent)
- **Mood:** ancient · patient · luminous
- **Key visual elements:**
  - Off-centre cross-section disc of a bristlecone pine, drawn as ~80 wavering concentric rings with a visible pith and a few scar-dark "frost rings."
  - Thin leader lines connecting four specific rings to small archival index-card annotations (ledger-style numerals + event label), set in the margin — the composition that fuses *tree* and *archive*.
  - Drafting-board context: grid, crop marks, scale bar, figure caption — the archivist's working surface.
- **Motion:** a single gold scanning arc (spring-driven) sweeps slowly from pith to bark, revealing annotation cards one by one as it crosses each dated ring; the rings themselves fade in from centre outward in the first second.
