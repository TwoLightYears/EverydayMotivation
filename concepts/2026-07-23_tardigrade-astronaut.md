# The Micronaut

**Pairing:** Tardigrade (*Ramazzottius varieornatus*) as Astronaut

## The hook
Dehydrated into a barrel-shaped "tun," tardigrades launched aboard the FOTON-M3 mission survived ten days of open-space exposure — hard vacuum plus unfiltered solar UV — and rehydrated back to life on Earth (Jönsson et al., *Current Biology* 18, 2008). They achieve this in part through **Dsup** (Damage suppressor), a nucleosome-binding protein that shrouds their DNA and cuts X-ray double-strand breaks by roughly 40% (Hashimoto et al., *Nature Communications* 2016).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#050716` — deep-space indigo (backdrop)
  - `#0F1738` — space blue (mid-field)
  - `#E9B871` — tardigrade tun amber (the animal itself)
  - `#F2E6C9` — pale cream (highlight, type primary)
  - `#D64545` — mission-badge red (accent)
  - `#7B8399` — cool instrument gray (grid, secondary type)
- **Mood:** clinical-yet-cosmic, mission-briefing, reverent
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A single tardigrade rendered head-on as a scientific-illustration silhouette — segmented body, four pairs of stubby legs with terminal claws, mouth stylet — floating dead-centre in a portrait window as though photographed through an EVA porthole.
  - A ringed "mission patch" halo around it — thin concentric orbits with tick marks calling out FOTON-M3 · LEO · 258 KM · 10 D · UV+VAC.
  - A star-field backdrop, sparse and pale.
  - Bottom-left type block: uppercase ROLE tag, italic display title, single-line hook.
- **Motion:** the tardigrade rotates slowly on its own axis (one revolution over ~6s) while the orbital rings' tick marks tick forward one notch per beat. Stars twinkle at low amplitude. A 6-second seamless loop.
