# The Sommelier of the Arctic

**Pairing:** Narwhal as Sommelier

## The hook
The narwhal's 2–3 m spiralling tusk is a modified left canine with roughly 10 million nerve endings reaching the dentin tubules; a porous outer cementum lets seawater seep through to those nerves, turning the tusk into a chemosensory probe that reads salinity, temperature, pressure and dissolved chemistry (Nweeia et al., *The Anatomical Record* 297:599–617, 2014). It tastes the ocean the way a sommelier tastes a glass — one slow, deliberate draw at a time.

## Visual brief
- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait)
- **Palette (drawn from narwhal + Arctic coloration):**
  - `#07111F` — abyssal navy (background)
  - `#13273F` — mid Arctic water
  - `#A9C3DD` — glacier blue (tusk highlight / UI)
  - `#F1E6CF` — tusk ivory
  - `#D9A26A` — warm cream / sommelier gold (dial pointer, "tasted" accent)
  - `#3C5168` — narwhal mottling / grid
- **Mood:** contemplative, cold-precise, patrician
- **Key visual elements:**
  - Focal object: the spiralled tusk rendered as a long tapered diagonal with left-handed helical grooves, drawn like a technical specimen plate.
  - Sommelier's signature prop: a four-axis "tasting wheel" at the tusk's tip (SALINITY · TEMPERATURE · PRESSURE · CHEMISTRY) with concentric gauge rings and a lone gold pointer settling on a reading.
  - Fusion idea: the tusk is the probe; the wheel is what it tastes. A whisker-thin cross-section inset at the base shows the dentin as a tasting glass — nerve-fascicle dots standing in for sediment in wine.
- **Motion:** the tusk draws in from the left along its helical axis; concentric gauge rings bloom outward from the tip; the gold pointer eases into position on the salinity dial — one single deliberate "tasting" gesture, spring-eased, under 3 seconds, then a soft 4 s loop pulse on the rings.
