# The 30,000-Kelvin Archivist

**Pairing:** Lightning as Archivist

## The hook
A cloud-to-ground return stroke drives a channel of ionized air to roughly 30,000 K — about five times the surface of the Sun — and where that channel grounds through quartz-rich sand, it instantly fuses the silica (melting point ~1,713 °C) into a hollow, branched glass tube called a *fulgurite* that preserves the bolt's underground path, down to its finest tendrils, for thousands of years (Pasek, Block & Pasek, *Contributions to Mineralogy and Petrology* 164, 477–492, 2012).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#07091A` — storm indigo (sky, deep negative space)
  - `#E8EEFF` — ion-channel white (lightning core, hottest highlight)
  - `#7E8CFF` — electric periwinkle (bolt halo, mid-glow)
  - `#D9B072` — quartz sand ochre (sand strata above the horizon of the fulgurite)
  - `#5C4B36` — fulgurite glass-brown (the fossilised tube itself)
- **Mood:** thunderous, archival, geologic, patient
- **Key visual elements:**
  - The frame is split by a horizon that is also a timeline: above it, a single branching lightning bolt caught at its brightest instant; below it, in the cross-section of the sand, the same branching path *mirrored and darkened* — the glassy fulgurite tube, drawn like a specimen plate with a labelled scale bar.
  - A faint grid behind the sand gives the composition the air of a geologist's log; small tick marks mark depth in centimetres.
  - Lower-left type block: the role tag, the display title, the hook, and the citation.
- **Motion:** a cold flash — the bolt strikes in a single spring on roughly frame 15, holds for a few frames at full brightness, then dims by half. Immediately after, the fulgurite "etches" downward along the same path with a cubic ease-out from the strike point to the deepest tendril — the archive being written. A 5 s loop.
