# The Lightning's Court Reporter

**Pairing:** Fulgurite as Stenographer

## The hook
When cloud-to-ground lightning strikes silica-rich sand or soil, the return-stroke channel briefly reaches plasma temperatures on the order of 30,000 K — hotter than the surface of the Sun — fusing grains along the current's exact path into a hollow glassy tube (a *fulgurite*) that preserves the branching geometry of the discharge as a physical, in-place transcript (Uman, *Lightning*, 1969; Pasek & Block, *Geology*, 2009).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 x 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0B0716` — storm-sky violet-black (upper sky / negative space)
  - `#D6B77A` — warm desert-sand ochre (substrate)
  - `#8A7C68` — mineral gray (charred sand halo, secondary type)
  - `#F6C453` — molten silica interior (fulgurite core glow)
  - `#E9F1FF` — plasma flash white-blue (lightning stroke, hot highlight)
- **Mood:** hushed, forensic, high-voltage stillness
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A vertical cross-section: sky above, sand below, split by a strict horizon line low in the frame.
  - A single lightning bolt striking the horizon, with a matching *branching* fulgurite tube descending into the sand — the sand-side tube mirroring the sky-side stroke, glowing at the core like it's just cooling from molten.
  - A thin stenographer's rule grid marking depth ticks (0, 1, 2 m) alongside the tube.
  - Type block in the lower band: uppercase role tag, italic display title, single-line hook.
- **Motion:** one primary event — a spring-driven lightning strike at ~1.0 s (single hot flash), then the fulgurite tube reveals from the strike point downward with a spring easing (top-to-bottom stroke-dashoffset), the molten core glow slowly fading from white-hot to amber over ~3 s. Calm 5 s loop.
