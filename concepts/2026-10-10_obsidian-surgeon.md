# The Glass That Cuts Between Cells

**Pairing:** Obsidian as Surgeon

## The hook
Because volcanic glass has no crystal grain to arrest a crack, a conchoidally-fractured obsidian flake can hold an edge whose tip radius is on the order of 3 nm (~30 Å) — roughly a hundred times finer than a stainless-steel scalpel, and the reason obsidian blades have been used in experimental ophthalmic and cardiothoracic incisions (Buck, *West J Med* 1982; Disa et al., *Ann Plast Surg* 1993).

## Visual brief

**Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait).

**Palette** (drawn from real obsidian coloration — predominantly amorphous black with mahogany striations and the faint blue–violet of rainbow obsidian):
- `#09090C` — volcanic void (deep near-black)
- `#1C1D23` — basalt shadow
- `#B9BEC8` — surgical-steel highlight
- `#D45530` — lava ember rim (the still-hot edge of fresh glass)
- `#5B7FA6` — rainbow-obsidian iridescence

**Mood:** precise · dangerous · quiet.

**Key visual elements:**
- Focal object: a single obsidian flake blade, asymmetric, with concentric conchoidal (shell-like) fracture ripples radiating from the strike point.
- Role prop: a surgeon's technical annotation — a fine leader line from the blade's leading edge out to a measurement callout, "r ≈ 30 Å," rendered in draftsman's type beside a nanometre scale bar.
- Composition that fuses them: a diagonal blade laid on a dark surgical drafting plate, treated as if it were an operating-theatre instrument plate — one object, one measurement, one argument.

**Motion:** one primary easing-driven motion — a specular glint traces the blade's leading edge from heel to tip while a soft wave of conchoidal ripples radiates outward from the strike point. Both ease out on a spring; the composition settles into a hero still.
