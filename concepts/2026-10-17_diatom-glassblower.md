# The ambient-temperature glassblower

**Pairing:** Diatom as Glassblower

## The hook

A diatom builds its own hull by secreting silaffin and long-chain polyamine proteins that polymerise dissolved silicic acid into an SiO₂·nH₂O (opaline glass) shell — a **frustule** — at ~15 °C seawater, patterning its pores on a 40–200 nm scale that industrial glassmakers can only approach by melting silica at **~1600 °C** (Kröger, Deutzmann & Sumper, *Science* 286, 1999; Hildebrand, *Chem. Rev.* 108, 2008).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 portrait.
- **Palette (drawn from the real coloration of a Coscinodiscus / Thalassiosira pillbox diatom under darkfield illumination, where the silica shell diffracts cold while fucoxanthin pigment reads golden):**
  - `#06202A` — deep ocean ink (background)
  - `#0E3540` — mid-column teal (vignette)
  - `#D6EEE7` — silica glass white (frustule, type)
  - `#9BD5CF` — cool rim highlight (pore shadows, structural lines)
  - `#D89844` — fucoxanthin amber (crop marks, accent)
- **Mood:** quietly scientific, luminous, hushed.
- **Key visual elements:** a single centred Coscinodiscus-style diatom disc, built from a true hexagonal pore lattice bounded by concentric silica rings and radiating costae. The one composition idea that fuses object and role: the frustule is drawn as if lit from behind on a glassblower's working bench — a plate of patterned glass, scaled, dimensioned, and labelled with the ambient temperature at which it was built.
- **Motion:** a single easing-driven light sweep passes left-to-right across the disc, igniting the hexagonal pores from the rim inward as if the glass were being pulled into shape — one primary gesture, 4 s, spring/cubic ease, no linear motion.
