# The Feathered Foley Artist

**Pairing:** Superb Lyrebird (*Menura novaehollandiae*) as Foley Artist

## The hook
A single male superb lyrebird can weave the songs of a dozen other bird species — and, in some populations, mechanical sounds such as camera shutters and chainsaws — into one continuous mimicry medley. The copies are so accurate that in playback experiments the mimicked species respond as if to their own kind (Dalziell & Magrath, *Animal Behaviour* 83 (2012): 1401–1410).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0F0A08` — deep umber studio ground / negative space
  - `#3A2418` — bark-shadow inks
  - `#8A4C24` — chestnut lyrebird body
  - `#D9A66A` — warm ochre plumage highlight
  - `#EBDDBE` — cream lyre-plume filaments / high-key type
- **Mood:** hushed, virtuosic, cinematic (a recording booth after hours)
- **Key visual elements:**
  - Multi-track "session strip": five stacked, labelled waveforms — KOOKABURRA, EASTERN WHIPBIRD, ROSELLA, CAMERA SHUTTER, CHAINSAW — laid out like a foley DAW.
  - The signature prop: a studio condenser microphone, framed as if pointed at the singer.
  - The lyrebird itself in silhouette at frame right — its diagnostic fanned "lyre" tail-plume forming the composition's counterweight to the track stack. Beak points toward the microphone; the tracks read as its take being captured.
- **Motion:** waveforms scroll leftward under a fixed playhead, as if being live-captured; a small REC dot pulses on beat, and the lyrebird's throat/beak gives a subtle rise-and-fall as it "sings." A ~5-second seamless loop.
