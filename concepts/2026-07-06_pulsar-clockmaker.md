# The Dead Star That Keeps Perfect Time

**Pairing:** Millisecond Pulsar as Master Clockmaker

## The hook
A millisecond pulsar — the ultradense collapsed core of a dead massive star — spins up to ~700 times per second and emits a radio beam whose pulse-arrival times are stable to about one part in 10¹⁵, so precise that arrays of these dead stars (NANOGrav, EPTA, PPTA) function as a galaxy-scale reference clock and, in 2023, revealed the nanohertz gravitational-wave background rippling through the Milky Way.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#05070E` — deep space ink (background, negative space)
  - `#0F1F3A` — cool navy shadow (dial well)
  - `#3D8BFD` — pulsar-beam electric blue (synchrotron)
  - `#E8F1FF` — bone-white beam highlight / neutron-star flash
  - `#D4A65A` — burnished chronometer brass (hairlines, dial ticks, role tag)
- **Mood:** precise, cosmic, timeworn
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A large brass chronometer dial etched into deep space — concentric rings, minor tick every 6°, major every 30°.
  - At the pivot: a brilliant white neutron-star pinprick with a soft cyan halo.
  - Two opposing lighthouse beams (pulsar radiation cones) emerge from the pivot and act as the dial's hands, sweeping through the brass ticks.
  - Below the dial: a metronomically regular pulse-profile train — the pulsar's time-of-arrival plot — echoing the composition idea that fuses object and role: **the dial is the clock, the star is the movement.**
  - Type block below the dial: brass ROLE tag ("MASTER CLOCKMAKER"), Playfair italic display title, single-line hook in Inter.
- **Motion:** the twin beams enter with a spring easing, rotating from ~-45° into their locked orientation while the dial ticks fade in around them; once locked, a perfectly regular pulse train marches across the bottom strip in tempo, one bright pulse per beam sweep. 5-second loop.
