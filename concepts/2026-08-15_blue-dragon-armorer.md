# The Armorer Who Steals Every Weapon

**Pairing:** Blue Dragon Nudibranch (*Glaucus atlanticus*) as Armorer

## The hook
*Glaucus atlanticus* eats Portuguese man o' war tentacles and, through a process called kleptocnidae, keeps the ingested nematocysts (stinging capsules) intact through its gut, routes them along ciliated cnidophage cells, and stockpiles the most potent ones inside cnidosacs at the very tips of its cerata — an internal armory of stolen weapons it fires in its own defense.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 x 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:** (drawn from the animal's real countershading and cerata tips)
  - `#040E24` — abyss (deep-sea substrate / negative space)
  - `#0B1F4C` — dorsal cobalt (the shadowed body side)
  - `#1E62E6` — electric ceras blue (the animal's signature stripe)
  - `#8FC0F5` — pearlescent highlight (silver-white belly / cnidosac glow)
  - `#F2C36B` — brass label ink (armorer's technical annotations, ammo tag)
- **Mood:** exacting, blueprint-quiet, faintly ominous
- **Key visual elements:**
  - The Blue Dragon rendered as a symmetric top-down technical plate — central body axis with three paired ceras clusters fanning out, each ceras terminating in a small circular cnidosac.
  - Overhead: a Portuguese man o' war float and a single trailing tentacle threading down toward the body — the supply line the armorer taps.
  - Small nematocyst-harpoon glyphs travelling from the tentacle, along the gut axis, out through each ceras, and accumulating in the cnidosacs at the tips.
  - A brass rack of technical callouts on the right ("CNIDOSAC", "CERAS", "GUT DUCT") with fine leader lines.
- **Motion:** one primary easing-driven motion — stolen nematocysts flow from the tentacle down the central gut axis, then branch outward along each ceras with a spring-eased delay, filling the cnidosacs at the tips (which glow up); once fully loaded, a single outer ceras discharges a nematocyst to seaward. A 5-second loop.
