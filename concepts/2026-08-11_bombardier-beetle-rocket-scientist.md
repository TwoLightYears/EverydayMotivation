# The Pulsejet Beetle

**Pairing:** Bombardier Beetle (*Brachinus* spp., *Stenaptinus insignis*) as Rocket Scientist

## The hook

To repel attackers, the bombardier beetle floods a reinforced abdominal reaction chamber with hydroquinones and ~25% hydrogen peroxide, where catalases and peroxidases catalyse a fiercely exothermic reaction that ejects a boiling ~100 °C benzoquinone spray — not as a continuous stream, but as roughly **500 discrete pulses per second**, the same pulsed-combustion principle later studied in pulsejet propulsion (Dean, Aneshansley & Eisner, *Science* 248 (1990) 1219–1221; Beheshti & McIntosh, *Bioinspiration & Biomimetics* 2, 2007).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0B0A0E` — chitin black (substrate / beetle body)
  - `#B23A18` — carapace rust (legs, antennae, secondary annotation lines)
  - `#F26A1B` — combustion orange (jet core, hero accent)
  - `#FFD86A` — ignition yellow (heat flash / hotspot)
  - `#8A8F99` — draftsman gray (blueprint grid, secondary type)
- **Mood:** engineered, staccato, dangerous-yet-precise
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A profile-view beetle rendered as a technical patent-style illustration on a dark drafting board, its abdomen curled up and aimed rearward like a swivel-mounted turret nozzle.
  - A staccato jet erupting from the abdomen tip along a diagonal upward arc — a bright ignition flash at the origin and a train of decreasing puffs marking the individual pulses.
  - Fine leader-line callouts naming the reservoir, reaction chamber and nozzle — the beetle treated as a labelled engine cross-section.
- **Motion:** the beetle draws itself in from a blueprint outline (~1 s), then ignites — pulsed jet bursts fire in a rhythmic staccato (~6 legible pulses across the loop), each punctuated by a bright ignition flash at the abdomen tip. A 5-second loop.
