# The Flash Photographer

**Pairing:** Snapping Shrimp (*Alpheus heterochaelis*) as Flash Photographer

## The hook
When a snapping shrimp closes its asymmetric claw at roughly 25 m/s, the resulting cavitation bubble collapses with a measurable flash of light — sonoluminescence — lasting only tens of picoseconds and reaching inferred temperatures near 5,000 K (Lohse et al., *Nature* 413 (2001): 477–478). Each snap fires an invisible flashbulb underwater.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 x 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette** (drawn from the shrimp's real coloration + the sonoluminescent flash):
  - `#060913` — abyssal ink (water column, background)
  - `#0F1B2E` — deep ocean navy (vignette mid-tone)
  - `#F0E4CE` — shrimp carapace cream
  - `#D64A2E` — snap-claw vermillion (the heavy claw's signature color)
  - `#7BC6FF` — sonoluminescence cyan (the picosecond flash)
  - `#8E96A3` — cool darkroom gray (type / technical annotation)
- **Mood:** still, forensic, electric — a darkroom recording an event almost too fast to witness
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A **contact-sheet / strip** composition: four sequential high-speed exposures of the same snap — loaded claw → mid-closure → cavitation bubble inflated → bubble collapse + sonoluminescent flash — laid on a dark darkroom frame.
  - Each exposure is a scientific illustration of the shrimp's business end, with a perforated edge mimicking 35 mm film sprockets.
  - A labelled technical annotation on the fourth frame (temperature, duration, sound level) — the "data caption" the photographer would scribble on the contact print.
  - Strong type below: ROLE tag, display title in serif italic, one-line hook.
- **Motion:** the four frames light up in sequence as if a shutter is advancing; on the final frame the cavitation bubble blooms and collapses, triggering a brief full-composition flash (cyan-white) that quickly settles back into the dark. A 5-second loop.
