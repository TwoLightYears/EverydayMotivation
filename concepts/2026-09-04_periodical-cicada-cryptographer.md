# The Prime-Numbered Cipher

**Pairing:** Periodical Cicada (*Magicicada*) as Cryptographer

## The hook
Periodical cicadas of the genus *Magicicada* emerge only on prime-numbered cycles — 13 or 17 years — an interval that keeps their generations from resonating with any shorter, non-prime predator population cycle (Cox & Carlton 1988; Yoshimura 1997; Goles, Schulz & Markus, *Complexity* 2001). The prime is a naturally evolved key: a co-emerging predator whose own cycle divides evenly into 12, 14, 15 or 16 years cannot phase-lock onto a 17-year brood for another 17 × (its cycle) years.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette** — drawn from *Magicicada septendecim*'s real coloration:
  - `#0F0B08` — deep bark / substrate ink
  - `#F4E3C0` — parchment / pale wing amber
  - `#C46A2C` — cicada wing-vein amber
  - `#A22222` — cicada compound-eye red
  - `#6B4423` — warm oak-brown mid-tone
- **Mood:** ceremonial, mathematical, patient
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A cipher-wheel dial marked 1 → 17 wraps around a centered cicada silhouette; ticks 13 and 17 (the two known prime cycles) are keyed in vein-amber, all other ticks are muted parchment.
  - The cicada's wing venation continues outward into the dial's rays, so the insect and the cryptographic mechanism read as one instrument.
  - Draftsman gridlines and a horizontal cross-hair sit behind the dial — the cryptographer's worksheet.
  - Type block: uppercase ROLE tag ("CRYPTOGRAPHER"), display title "The Prime-Numbered Cipher," single-line hook.
- **Motion:** the dial rotates one tick at a time along a spring easing; on the 13th and 17th ticks the prime keys light up (vein-amber flash → hold), and the cicada's wings unfurl on tick 17. Roughly a 5-second beat.
