# Steered by the Galaxy

**Pairing:** Dung Beetle (*Scarabaeus satyrus*) as Celestial Navigator

## The hook
On moonless Kalahari nights, the African dung beetle *Scarabaeus satyrus* climbs atop its freshly rolled ball, performs a brief 360° "dance" to take a mental snapshot of the sky, then rolls in a perfectly straight line — using the diffuse band of the **Milky Way itself** as a compass. It is the first non-human animal proven to navigate by our galaxy (Dacke, Baird, Byrne, Scholtz & Warrant, *Current Biology* 23:298–300, 2013).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 x 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#08091A` — deep Kalahari night sky (substrate / negative space)
  - `#F1E4B8` — warm galactic glow / bright stars
  - `#2D3350` — twilight indigo (mid-sky gradient, sightline highlight)
  - `#C8935A` — Kalahari sand / dung-ball earth
  - `#7C8496` — cool navigator gray (grid, type secondary, dashed guides)
- **Mood:** hushed, cosmic, precise
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A grand arch of the **Milky Way** curving across the upper two-thirds of the frame, dense with stippled stars and a bright galactic core.
  - A tiny beetle silhouetted on sand at the bottom, in top-down plan view, its round dung ball a step ahead of it.
  - A fine dashed **sight-line** rising vertically from the beetle to a precise point on the galactic band — the "reading."
  - A perfectly straight **trajectory line** across the sand, rendered as a chart's bearing vector, perpendicular to the band's local tangent.
  - Lower-left type lockup: uppercase ROLE tag, an italic serif display title ("Steered by the galaxy."), and a single hook paragraph.
- **Motion:** stars ignite first, then the Milky Way fades in as a soft luminous arch; the sight-line draws upward from beetle to sky; a brief "snapshot" pulse blooms where the sight meets the band; finally the bearing line traces across the sand along the beetle's true course. A single calm 5-second reveal.
