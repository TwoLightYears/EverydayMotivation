# The 17-Year Proof

**Pairing:** Periodical Cicada (*Magicicada septendecim*) as Number Theorist

## The hook

*Magicicada septendecim* spends exactly **17 years** underground before emerging in a single deafening brood — and 17 is prime, so for any hypothetical predator with a cycle *p* between 2 and 16 years, their broods next coincide only after **lcm(17, *p*) = 17*p*** years. A prime life-cycle is the longest possible stagger a cicada can hold against a shorter-cycled enemy; sister species *M. tredecim* uses the other nearby prime, 13, and no observed brood uses a composite period. (Yoshimura, *Am. Nat.* 1997; Goles, Schulz & Markus, *Complexity* 2001.)

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350, portrait.
- **Palette:**
  - `#0E1216` — slate ink (background)
  - `#17221C` — chalkboard dark green (specimen plate)
  - `#EDE4C7` — chalk cream (numerals, rules)
  - `#C97B34` — cicada wing amber (venation, primes)
  - `#B72B22` — cicada eye red (the number 17, downbeat)
- **Mood:** exacting, quiet, hymnbook-scientific.
- **Key visual elements:**
  - Focal object: a dorsal-view *Magicicada* silhouette rendered in amber wing-venation strokes.
  - Role's signature prop: a **17-tick dial** — a chalkboard number-wheel of years 1–17, ticks arranged at 360/17°, primes struck in amber and composites in muted chalk.
  - Fusing composition: the cicada is centered *inside* the dial, so the ring of years reads like an orbital timekeeper around a body that keeps that time.
- **Motion:** a single pointer sweeps the dial year-by-year with a snap-easing (a metronome, not linear), lighting primes as it passes and landing on **17** in cicada-eye red, held. The cicada's wings pick up the same amber as the year lands.
