# The Parachute That Leaks

**Pairing:** Dandelion Seed as Aerospace Engineer

## The hook
A dandelion seed drifts for kilometres because its pappus — a crown of roughly 100 fine bristles with a porosity of **0.916** — lets most of the air pass straight through, and in doing so generates a **separated vortex ring (SVR)**: a stable, detached torus of recirculating flow sitting above the pappus that gives the seed about **four times the drag of a solid disc of the same area** (Cummins et al., *Nature* 562 (2018) 414–418).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette** — drawn from a real dandelion seedhead photographed against twilight:
  - `#0F1522` — twilight sky (substrate / negative space)
  - `#F6F2E6` — pappus white (bristles, type)
  - `#D8CFBB` — pappus mid-tone (secondary bristles, SVR arcs)
  - `#C9A066` — achene / seed-body warm tan (focal accent)
  - `#E8C972` — pollen gold (role tag, highlights)
  - `#5B6B7E` — cool slate (grid, secondary labels)
- **Mood:** airborne, exacting, drifting-but-engineered
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A single dandelion seed, achene at the bottom and pappus fanning upward, scaled to fill most of the central stage.
  - Above the pappus — floating like a halo — a traced **separated vortex ring**: two concentric arcs rendered as a torus in side profile, the aerodynamic signature of the hook.
  - Streamlines flowing from below, curling around the achene, threading through the porous pappus, and curling into the SVR.
  - Technical callouts in the margins: `~100 BRISTLES`, `POROSITY 0.916`, `4× DRAG OF SOLID DISC` — the seed treated as a page from a flight-test workbook.
  - A faint drafting grid behind the stage; crop marks and a wind-direction arrow at the lower edge.
- **Motion:** one primary motion — the streamlines flow from bottom to top on a continuous dash-offset cycle, and the vortex ring breathes (slow radial pulse) in a 4–5 s loop. The seed itself drifts in on a soft spring at the top of the composition.
