# The Forest Floor's Foley Artist

**Pairing:** Superb Lyrebird (*Menura novaehollandiae*) as Foley Artist

## The hook
The male superb lyrebird's winter territorial song is dominated by mimicry — roughly 70–80% of it — with a documented repertoire that can include the calls of 20+ other bird species (kookaburras, cockatoos, rosellas, kingfishers) and, in landscapes touched by people, the mechanical sounds around him: camera shutters, motor drives, car alarms, chainsaws, even barking dogs — all stitched into a single performance from a raised earthen mound.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette (drawn from real lyrebird coloration):**
  - `#0F0C09` — deep umber (forest-floor negative space)
  - `#2B2018` — undertone brown (panel shading)
  - `#7C4A2A` — cinnamon body brown (the bird itself)
  - `#B8894B` — golden bronze (lyrate feather rachis, role accent)
  - `#EFE4CE` — warm ivory (filamentary plumes, primary type)
  - `#8B7458` — draftsman warm-gray (secondary type, tick marks)
- **Mood:** theatrical, precise, hushed — like a field guide plate crossed with a mixing-desk track sheet
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A small stylised silhouette of the male lyrebird at the base of the composition, in profile, beak open in song, standing on a low earthen display mound.
  - **The one composition idea that fuses object and role:** his fanned tail rendered as a *fan of catalogued sound sources* — each of the 15 tail feathers tagged at its tip with what the bird can sing back (KOOKABURRA, CHAINSAW, CAMERA SHUTTER, CAR ALARM, HUMAN VOICE …). The two outermost feathers are drawn as the ornate lyrate feathers with their real curved-hook shape; the inner feathers are the filamentary silver plumes rendered as fine radiating strokes — the lyrebird's actual tail literally reading as an inventory of samples.
  - A single delicate "staff line" arcing through the fan mid-radius — a musical-score reference tying the technical feel together.
- **Motion:** the tail unfurls outward from the center in a staggered spring, feather by feather; once open, a bronze "playhead" pulse sweeps once across the arc (dramatising playback), each label lighting as the head passes it. A 5-second loop.
