# Blown by the Sky

**Pairing:** Fulgurite as Glassblower

## The hook
When cloud-to-ground lightning strikes silica-rich sand, its return-stroke plasma channel briefly reaches ~30,000 K — roughly five times hotter than the Sun's photosphere (5,778 K) — and in milliseconds fuses the sand along its path into a branching, hollow tube of amorphous silica glass called a fulgurite.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 x 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0E0C14` — storm ink (sky, deep background)
  - `#B9D4FF` — lightning blue-white (bolt core, plasma)
  - `#FFE9A8` — forge glow (molten silica at formation)
  - `#D4B084` — sand tan (dune surface, cooled crust)
  - `#5E3C22` — buried earth / deep glass shadow
- **Mood:** forged, alchemical, hushed-after-thunder
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A single jagged lightning bolt descending into a sand horizon that cuts the frame roughly 40/60 (sky above, ground cross-section below).
  - Below the strike point: a branching, dendritic fulgurite tube in cross-section — the lightning's ghost in glass — glowing hot where freshly fused, cooling to tan/amber at the branch tips.
  - Field-notebook framing: thin crop marks, a fine inner border, a caption strip underneath that labels the figure.
  - Type lockup bottom-left (asymmetric): small uppercase ROLE tag, italic Playfair title, Inter hook paragraph.
- **Motion:** A spring-eased descent of the bolt from the top of the frame to the strike point, followed by a radial flash at impact; the fulgurite then extrudes downward in delay-ordered segments; finally the hot forge-glow cools (gradient shifts from white→yellow→amber→muted tan) along the branches. ~5 s loop.
