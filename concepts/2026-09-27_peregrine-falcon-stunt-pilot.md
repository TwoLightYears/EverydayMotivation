# The 389-Kilometre-an-Hour Test Pilot

**Pairing:** Peregrine Falcon (*Falco peregrinus*) as Stunt Pilot

## The hook
In a hunting stoop, *Falco peregrinus* reaches roughly **389 km/h (242 mph)** — the fastest recorded speed of any animal, measured by Ken Franklin in 2005 by skydiving alongside a trained falcon named Frightful. To keep breathing at that airspeed the bird's nares carry small bony **tubercles**, geometrically similar to the inlet cones used on supersonic jet intakes to slow incoming air.

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette** (drawn from real *Falco peregrinus* coloration):
  - `#0B1220` — twilight-sky ink (background / negative space)
  - `#F2C46A` — cere / eye-ring yellow (signal-orange accent)
  - `#E5DFCF` — pale breast cream
  - `#6C4A2B` — warm sepia of head-cap and back
  - `#8FA3B8` — cool draftsman slate (grid, secondary type, wind lines)
- **Mood:** aerodynamic, exacting, kinetic
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A stoop-diving peregrine rendered as a flight-manual schematic — tucked-wing teardrop silhouette angled ~62° from horizontal, drawn in linework with two flat fills (dark head/back, pale breast).
  - Overlay of aviation instrumentation: dive-angle protractor arc, wind streaks trailing the bird, altitude ticks along the right edge, and an airspeed dial in the corner reading **389 km/h**.
  - A **Detail A** magnified inset showing the nostril's bony tubercle in cross-section with vortex airflow lines — the "inlet-cone" hook made literal.
  - Strong type lockup below the schematic: an uppercase ROLE tag, a large italic display title, and a single-paragraph hook.
- **Motion:** the falcon springs into its dive from the upper margin along an ease-out curve; wind streaks stretch in behind it; the airspeed needle sweeps from 0 → 389; the Detail-A inset fades in last with its vortex lines curling. A 5-second beat.
