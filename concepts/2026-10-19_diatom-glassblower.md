# The Glassblower Who Works in the Cold

**Pairing:** Diatom as Glassblower

## The hook
Each of the roughly 100,000 species of diatom — a single-celled alga — builds its own species-signature silica shell, called a *frustule*, with nanometer precision, polymerizing dissolved silicic acid into intricate pored lattices with feature sizes of 40–200 nm using silaffin peptides at ambient seawater temperature (Kröger et al., *Science* 286, 1999). Industrial glassmakers need ≈1,600 °C to work silica; the diatom works at 4.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#061724` — abyssal ink (substrate / negative space)
  - `#0C3240` — kelp teal (plate / drafting board)
  - `#E6F0F2` — silica pale (frustule core)
  - `#79C3BF` — frustule aqua (highlight / inner glow)
  - `#D98936` — fucoxanthin amber (chloroplast / dimension marks)
- **Mood:** precise, luminous, microscopic
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A single radial diatom frustule (Actinoptychus / Arachnoidiscus archetype) centered on a dark drafting plate: concentric rings of hexagonal pores radiating from a wedge-divided hub, drawn as a crisp technical schematic.
  - Dimension callouts around the frustule — pore Ø, frustule Ø (µm), SiO₂ formula, 4 °C — set in uppercase micro-type.
  - Strong lower-left type block: display italic title, uppercase ROLE tag, and a single-line hook.
- **Motion:** silica polymerization — fine aqua strands draw outward from the hub along a spring easing, pores "ignite" sequentially with a soft amber glow, then a slow illumination arc sweeps around the plate once formed. A 5-second loop.
