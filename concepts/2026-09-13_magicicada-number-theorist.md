# The Prime-Number Savant

**Pairing:** 17-Year Cicada (*Magicicada septendecim*) as Number Theorist

## The hook
North America's two periodical cicada genera emerge en masse only after exactly **13** or **17** years underground — both prime numbers. The coprime cycle is thought to be an evolved predator-avoidance strategy: no predator whose population fluctuates on a 2-, 3-, 4-, or 5-year rhythm can lock into resonance with a 17-year brood, so the swarm's numeric period is itself the defence (Goles, Schulz & Markus, *Complexity* 6, 2001; Yoshimura, *American Naturalist* 149, 1997).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:** drawn from a live *M. septendecim* set on entomologist's paper
  - `#EFE3C6` — parchment ground
  - `#0B0A08` — chitin black / ink (type, cicada body)
  - `#B22222` — vermillion compound eye + prime numerals (primary accent)
  - `#E5A34A` — amber wing membrane
  - `#7A6A50` — soil-brown neutral (grid rules, composite numerals, secondary type)
- **Mood:** hushed, precise, almost liturgical — a proof laid out on parchment
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A 10 × 10 **Sieve of Eratosthenes** of the numbers 1–100, set on a warm cream ground. Composites are quietly greyed and slashed; primes glow amber.
  - Two illustrated cicadas perched on the cells **13** and **17** — the only numbers on the grid that get an occupant.
  - Marginal notation ("BROOD X · 17 YR", "BROOD XIX · 13 YR") in loose, engraver-style small caps; a footnote citation runs along the bottom.
- **Motion:** one primary sieve animation — the grid appears in a spring; composites strike-through in a soft wave from 2× outward; then the two cicadas alight on 13 and 17 with a brief wing-flutter and settle. A 5 s loop.
