# The Bird That Hushed a Train

**Pairing:** Common Kingfisher (*Alcedo atthis*) as Bullet-Train Designer

## The hook
When the JR-West 500 Series Shinkansen exited tunnels at 300 km/h, compressed air ahead of it created a "tunnel boom" audible for hundreds of metres; test engineer Eiji Nakatsu — an amateur birdwatcher — reprofiled the train's 15-metre nose after the common kingfisher's beak, whose gradient section lets the bird pierce the ~800× density jump from air into water almost without a splash. The redesigned nose eliminated the sonic boom, cut electricity use by ~15%, and let the train run about 10% faster.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0B1220` — deep tunnel-mouth midnight (background)
  - `#1FA6C6` — kingfisher iridescent crown turquoise
  - `#0E4A78` — deep kingfisher wing blue
  - `#F16B29` — kingfisher breast rusty orange
  - `#F5F7FA` — Shinkansen white / drafting paper
- **Mood:** precise, aerodynamic, quiet-explosive
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A large horizontal "engineering study": kingfisher head and beak on the LEFT resolving through a dashed transition zone into the Shinkansen 500 Series nose profile on the RIGHT — the same silhouette carried across species.
  - A drafting-board grid, technical annotations (chord length, half-angle, airflow arrows), and a set of concentric "shock" arcs at the bird's beak-tip that dampen and thin out at the train nose, dramatising the elimination of the boom.
  - Strong bottom-left type lockup: uppercase ROLE tag, large italic display title, single-line hook, footer citation.
- **Motion:** a single, spring-eased sweep — the shock arcs pulse outward from the beak tip, thinning to nothing by the time they reach the train nose (a 4-second loop). Title and hook fade in with a gentle stagger after the sweep.
