# The 500-Pulse Pulse Jet

**Pairing:** Bombardier Beetle (*Brachinus*) as Chemical Engineer

## The hook
The bombardier beetle carries a two-chamber liquid-fuel reactor in its abdomen: a reservoir of ~25% hydrogen peroxide and ~10% hydroquinones is metered into a hardened reaction chamber lined with catalase and peroxidase, whose exothermic decomposition to benzoquinone heats the mixture to ~100 °C and ejects it as a steam-driven spray pulsed at ~500 cycles per second — a biological pulse jet documented by Dean, Aneshansley, Edgerton & Eisner in *Science* 248 (1990): 1219–1221.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster).
- **Palette** (drawn from *Brachinus* coloration and hot-spray plume):
  - `#0B0D12` — near-black ink (background)
  - `#D14A28` — pronotum orange / spray core
  - `#F4A93A` — hot amber (plume outer, catalytic glow)
  - `#3B5A78` — cool steel-blue (elytra highlights, blueprint linework)
  - `#D8DFEA` — chalk (schematic annotations)
- **Mood:** precise, warm-industrial, technical-plate.
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A large, engineering-blueprint **cutaway of the beetle in strict profile**, elytra opened to reveal the two-chamber reactor: labelled **reservoir**, **muscular valve**, **reaction chamber** (with catalase / peroxidase noted on the wall), and the **rear nozzle**.
  - The role's signature prop: **numbered callouts, pressure/temperature dials, and chemical formulae** rendered as fine white linework on the dark ground — the beetle *is* a chemical-plant schematic.
  - A hot amber **spray plume** exiting the rear nozzle to the right, drawn as concentric arcs (each arc = one pulse of the 500 Hz jet).
- **Motion:** a single 4.5 s firing loop. Reactants stream from reservoir into reaction chamber, the reaction chamber flashes, then the plume ejects as a rapid train of arcs from the nozzle — one primary spring/easing motion that dramatizes the pulsed-jet mechanism.
