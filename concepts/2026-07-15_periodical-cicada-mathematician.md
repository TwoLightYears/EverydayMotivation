# The 17-Year Number Theorist

**Pairing:** Periodical Cicada (*Magicicada septendecim*) as Prime Number Mathematician

## The hook
*Magicicada septendecim* spends exactly 17 years underground as a nymph before mass-emerging in synchronized broods, and its sibling species run on 13-year clocks — both primes. Because 17 and 13 share no common factors with the 2-to-10-year population cycles of their bird, wasp, and fungal predators, no predator cycle can resonate with the emergence; the two prime broods themselves overlap only once every 221 years (13 × 17). Formalized by Cox & Carlton (1988) and Yoshimura (*American Naturalist*, 1997).

- **Mood:** patient, chthonic, mathematical
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A single dominant vertical "column of years" — an editorial numerical timeline listing 1 through 17. Composite primes are dim; primes 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13 glow softly; **17** is the terminal, oversized figure — where the cicada emerges.
  - A cross-section band running behind the column: a dark loam layer with a dormant nymph silhouette below the soil line, and an adult *Magicicada* emerging at the year-17 mark.
  - Editorial type block: uppercase ROLE tag, italic Playfair display title, single-line hook, taxonomic footer with the two prime coemergence math (13 × 17 = 221 yr).
- **Motion:** A single primary motion — the year counter ticks 1 → 17 on a spring easing, prime numbers illuminating as they pass, culminating at 17 with the adult cicada rising through the soil line and unfurling its amber-veined wings. Once at 17, a gentle pulse on the prime figures. ~5-second loop.
