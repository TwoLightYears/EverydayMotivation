# The Glassblower Who Etches at 45 Nanometres

**Pairing:** Diatom (*Coscinodiscus wailesii*) as Master Etcher

## The hook
The diatom *Coscinodiscus wailesii* secretes a three‑layered silica frustule in which the innermost sieve — the *cribellum* — is perforated by a hexagonal pore array with openings of roughly **45 nm**, patterned biologically to a fineness that begins to rival modern EUV photolithography (Losic, Rosengarten, Mitchell & Voelcker, *J. Porous Mater.* 14, 2007; Round, Crawford & Mann, *The Diatoms*, CUP 1990).

## Visual brief
- **Dimensions:** 1080×1350 portrait.
- **Palette (drawn from *Coscinodiscus* under bright‑field and DIC microscopy):**
  - `#070C15` — deep pelagic ink (background)
  - `#F2E8D0` — biogenic silica cream (opal frustule)
  - `#F5B942` — fucoxanthin gold (living chloroplast pigment)
  - `#4CB8C4` — iridescent teal (thin‑film interference on the shell)
  - `#A67C00` — oxidised bronze (mineral halo)
- **Mood:** precise, luminous, microscopic.
- **Key visual elements:**
  - **Focal object:** a top‑down cross‑section of a single *Coscinodiscus* frustule — a large disc whose surface reveals concentric zones of the hexagonal pore lattice at three scales.
  - **Signature prop of the role:** the visual grammar of a semiconductor photomask / reticle — alignment crosses, corner fiducials, nanometre‑scale bars, wafer notch and inspection callouts framing the specimen.
  - **Fusion idea:** the diatom is presented as a "biological reticle" on an inspection microscope — the shell IS the chip pattern, the ruler IS in nanometres, the technician's marks IS what draws the eye to the smallest ring.
- **Motion:** the hexagonal pores etch in from the centre outward on a spring‑eased scanline, then a callout leader snaps to the innermost 45 nm cribellum ring and holds — a single, calm reveal that dramatises the fineness of the pattern.
