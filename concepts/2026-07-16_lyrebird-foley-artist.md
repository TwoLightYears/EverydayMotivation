# The Peer-Reviewed Impersonator

**Pairing:** Superb Lyrebird (*Menura novaehollandiae*) as Foley Artist

## The hook
An adult male superb lyrebird spends roughly 72% of his territorial recital producing high-fidelity imitations of at least twenty other bird species — mimicry so faithful the spectrograms overlay onto the source calls — layered into displays that can run for twenty minutes (Zann & Dunstan, *Animal Behaviour* 76 (2008): 1043–1054).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 x 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#141009` — forest-floor umber (substrate / negative space)
  - `#3F2E20` — chestnut brown (outer lyrate feathers)
  - `#8A6338` — bronze feather-warmth (secondary accent)
  - `#C8B58C` — pale filamentary gold (spectrogram strings)
  - `#E9E1CE` — silvery cream (highlights / primary type)
- **Mood:** virtuosic, exacting, theatrical-in-the-understory
- **Key visual elements:**
  - Focal object: the lyrebird's fanned display-tail, rendered as a symmetrical lyre-form — two chestnut outer arms cradling ~24 vertical "strings."
  - The role's signature prop: those strings are audio spectrogram bars, and each vertical band is tagged with the species (or sound) being imitated — KOOKABURRA · WHIPBIRD · GREY SHRIKE-THRUSH · SATIN BOWERBIRD · CAMERA SHUTTER — turning the tail into a live studio readout.
  - Small compact bird silhouette anchored at the base of the lyre; a fine time-axis with tick marks; ROLE tag, italic display title, and a single-line hook set below on a strict two-column grid.
- **Motion:** the spectrogram strings paint up from the base with staggered spring easing (left-to-right, like a recording playhead sweeping through the imitation set); each species label fades in as its band completes. A calm 5-second loop.
