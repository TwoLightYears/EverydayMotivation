# The Deep-Sea Loom

**Pairing:** Hagfish as Textile Engineer

## The hook
Each of a hagfish's ~100 slime glands is packed with tens of thousands of specialised gland-thread cells (GTCs), and every single cell holds one continuous protein thread roughly 15 cm long and 1–3 μm thick, coiled inside the cell like a skein of silk — on release, the skeins unspool in under half a second and recruit seawater into a defensive slime up to ~10,000× the volume of the original exudate (Fudge et al., *Biology Letters* 2010; Lim et al., *Journal of Experimental Biology* 2006).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait)
- **Palette:**
  - `#070E16` — abyssal ink (background)
  - `#12314A` — deep-ocean blue (board / vignette)
  - `#E6DEC8` — ivory silk thread (signature filament)
  - `#F1EFEA` — pearl slime (highlights, type)
  - `#C86B6B` — hagfish mucosa coral (accent / role tag)
- **Mood:** patient, precise, quietly uncanny — a marine biologist's loom plate.
- **Key visual elements:** a technical "spec sheet" of a single gland-thread cell: a stylised round skein on the left side of a gridded navy plate, an ivory filament unspooling from it to the right along a measured scale bar with tick marks reading `0 cm` → `15 cm`, labelled callouts (`GLAND·THREAD·CELL`, `SKEIN`, `FILAMENT 1–3 μm`), and the hagfish rendered as a slender sinuous silhouette whose own body loops into the filament — the creature IS the thread.
- **Motion:** one primary spring-eased motion — the ivory filament unspools from the skein and extends along the scale bar over roughly 2.5 s, with the hagfish silhouette tracing the thread's path; the final 2 s of the loop holds the fully-extended spec plate steady so the hook reads.
