# The Patient Archivist

**Pairing:** Peat Bog as Archivist

## The hook
Sphagnum-fed peat bogs hold their water at roughly pH 3.5 with almost no dissolved oxygen, releasing sphagnan — a pectin-like polysaccharide that binds nitrogen and inactivates the enzymes microbes need to decompose tissue. The result is a cold, acidic, anaerobic vault: *Tollund Man*, found in a Danish bog in 1950, was radiocarbon-dated to roughly 405–380 BCE yet still preserved his skin, eyelashes, stubble, and the gruel from his last meal — about 2,400 years in cold storage.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#1A120B` — deep peat (near-black, cold, waterlogged)
  - `#4A2E18` — tannin-stained water / lower stratum
  - `#8C5A2B` — mid-peat ochre (older layers)
  - `#C9A063` — aged linen / archival label
  - `#5B6B36` — sphagnum green (surface moss)
- **Mood:** hushed, exacting, patient
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A vertical cross-section of a peat bog rendered as an archive — ten horizontal strata stacked like a card-catalog cabinet, each one a filing drawer edge with a sepia index label naming a year (0 CE, −500 BCE, −1000 BCE, down to −2500 BCE).
  - At one labeled depth, a small side-tag reads TOLLUND MAN / c. 400 BCE — the record pulled from storage.
  - A thin film of sphagnum green and standing tannin-tea water on top; faint bubbles of methane rising through the stack.
  - Draftsman grid underneath; title lockup lower-left with uppercase ROLE tag and one-line hook.
- **Motion:** a slow top-down reveal — the strata ink in from the surface downward on an ease-out cubic, each drawer-label fading in a half-beat after its stratum settles; the TOLLUND MAN tag slides out from its drawer last, with a gentle hover. A single 5-second loop.
