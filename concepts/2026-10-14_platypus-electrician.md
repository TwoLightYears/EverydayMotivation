# The Mammal With a Multimeter

**Pairing:** Platypus (*Ornithorhynchus anatinus*) as Electrician

## The hook
The platypus hunts eyes-closed in muddy river bottom with a bill wired like a diagnostic probe: roughly 40,000 electroreceptors and 60,000 push‑rod mechanoreceptors tiled across its leathery rostrum, letting it localize prey from the sub‑millivolt muscle twitches of shrimp and insect larvae — the first electroreception documented in a mammal (Scheich, Langner, Tidemann, Coles & Guppy, *Nature* 319, 401–402, 1986).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait social poster)
- **Palette** (drawn from the animal's real coloration and the muddy water it hunts in):
  - `#0B141A` — deep river‑bottom ink (ground)
  - `#1A2B33` — silt teal (water body)
  - `#3B2A1F` — platypus pelage (brown‑black velvet fur)
  - `#C78A52` — bill / webbing tan (keratinous rostrum)
  - `#5EE2FF` — electric blue (voltage trace, the hook's cue)
  - `#F3E7D1` — bone cream (type, hairlines)
- **Mood:** quiet, instrumented, slightly uncanny — a nature plate overlaid with wiring‑schematic logic.
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A side‑profile platypus, head tilted downward into the silt, eyes closed. The bill is the compositional pivot.
  - Electrician's signature prop: a schematic halo of voltage‑probe arcs radiating from the bill — fine blue traces that terminate on tiny prey silhouettes (shrimp, larvae) scattered on the substrate, each with a measured μV reading.
  - A thin blueprint grid under the water, hair‑cross reticles on three prey hits, and the title/role tag anchored lower‑left on a strict margin.
- **Motion:** a single primary motion — a sweep of electric‑blue probe lines fans from the bill in a spring easing, locking onto each prey target with a soft spark; micro‑voltage labels then type‑on. 5‑second loop.
