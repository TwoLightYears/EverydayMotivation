# The Nocturnal Navigator

**Pairing:** Dung Beetle (*Scarabaeus satyrus*) as Astronomer

## The hook
The nocturnal South African dung beetle *Scarabaeus satyrus* uses the diffuse band of the Milky Way itself — not individual stars — as a compass to roll its dung ball in a straight line away from the pile; under a planetarium sky with the galactic band blacked out, its paths deteriorate into loops (Dacke et al., *Current Biology* 23: 298–300, 2013). It is the first non-human animal shown to navigate by the galaxy.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#060915` — deep night sky (background)
  - `#121A33` — upper-atmosphere blue (sky gradient top)
  - `#F2EEDD` — Milky Way cream band
  - `#B8995A` — dung-ball amber (role-prop accent)
  - `#D4B870` — dung-ball highlight / warm specular
  - `#5A6478` — cool stellar gray (secondary type, reticle lines)
- **Mood:** cosmic, humble, exacting
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A soft, diffuse Milky Way band arcing across the upper two-thirds of the sky, rendered as a creamy nebular glow over a stippled star field.
  - A single compact dung beetle silhouette, viewed in profile, pushing a textured dung ball along a horizon line that cuts the frame roughly 60% down.
  - A straight dashed course line stretching the width of the horizon — the beetle's bearing — ticked with past positions to dramatize the straight-line result of galactic navigation.
  - A small bearing reticle lower-right locking the beetle's heading onto the galactic vector overhead.
  - Lower type block: Role tag (ASTRONOMER), display title "The nocturnal / navigator.", and single-line hook. Strong hierarchy, left-aligned, respects margin grid.
- **Motion:** the beetle translates slowly right along the horizon while the dung ball rotates on its contact point (spring-eased); a soft "fix" pulse travels from the Milky Way band down into the bearing reticle each cycle; stars twinkle at low amplitude. A 5-second loop.
