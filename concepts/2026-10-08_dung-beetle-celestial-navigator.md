# The Beetle Who Steers by the Galaxy

**Pairing:** Dung Beetle (*Scarabaeus satyrus*) as Celestial Navigator

## The hook
*Scarabaeus satyrus* — a thumb-sized South African dung beetle — orients the straight-line roll of its dung ball to the diffuse band of the Milky Way, the first animal of any kind shown to use the galaxy itself as a compass (Dacke, Baird, Byrne, Scholtz & Warrant, *Current Biology* 23 (4), 2013). In the planetarium trials, beetles under a full starfield and beetles under *only* the Milky Way rolled with the same tight dispersion; under a diffuse cloudy sky they walked in circles.

## Visual brief
- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 portrait.
- **Palette:** `#060814` (void), `#0F1530` (midnight), `#F2E6B9` (Milky Way cream), `#C9A86A` (starlight brass), `#7A4F2A` (dung umber), `#D3C6A0` (parchment ink). Pulled from the actual night-sky colours and the matte-brown dung ball Scarabaeus rolls.
- **Mood:** reverent, chart-like, quiet-cosmic.
- **Key visual elements:** a long dust-lane Milky Way arcing across the upper two-thirds of the frame; a plumb line of ticks descending from the galactic band down to a tiny silhouetted beetle on a dung ball at the bottom of the plate; graticule ticks and bearing marks framing the composition like a 19th-century celestial navigation chart. The beetle's roll vector points straight up at the galactic axis — the single composition idea that fuses creature to cosmos.
- **Motion:** the Milky Way drifts slowly left-to-right (the apparent rotation of the sky), the plumb line extends downward on a spring, stars twinkle in low amplitude. Calm, intentional, 5 s loop.
