# The Living Index

**Pairing:** Bristlecone Pine (*Pinus longaeva*) as Archivist

## The hook
Great Basin bristlecone pines lay down exactly one narrow growth ring per year and preserve them for millennia in dense, resin-saturated wood — Edmund Schulman crossdated 4,789 rings in the "Methuselah" tree in California's White Mountains in 1957, and matched dead standing wood extends the continuous bristlecone chronology past 9,000 years.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 x 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0F1210` — deep ink (charred / archive-vault background)
  - `#C6B99A` — weathered silver-bone heartwood
  - `#B57935` — warm amber ring (living cambium tone)
  - `#E9B865` — highlight glow (illuminated ring)
  - `#3F4F3D` — deep pine-needle green (index tick / accent)
- **Mood:** ancient, patient, monumental
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A large circular cross-section of the bristlecone, off-set right, drawn as dense concentric growth rings — narrow near the pith, thicker at the margin, subtly modulated year-to-year.
  - The archivist's signature prop: a column of hand-lettered index-card annotations down the left margin, each with a leader line pointing precisely to a specific ring dated to a real historical event (e.g. 2832 BCE — pith; 776 BCE — first Olympiad; 1215 CE — Magna Carta; 1957 CE — Schulman's core).
  - Fusion: the tree's ring-slice IS the archive — every filament of xylem an entry in a 4,858-year ledger.
- **Motion:** a single primary sweep — a warm amber highlight travels once inward across the rings from the outermost (2026) to the pith (2832 BCE), triggering each index annotation as it passes, then holds on the hero still.
