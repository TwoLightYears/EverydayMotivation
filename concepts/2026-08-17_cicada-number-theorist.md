# The Prime-Number Insect

**Pairing:** Periodical Cicada (*Magicicada*) as Number Theorist

## The hook
The periodical cicadas of eastern North America spend either **13** or **17** years underground before erupting en masse — both intervals are prime, so a predator with any short population cycle of 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6 years can never phase-lock to the emergence. Two co-occurring broods of different periods would only coincide once every 13 × 17 = **221 years** (Yoshimura, *American Naturalist* 149, 1997; Goles, Schulz & Markus, *Complexity* 6, 2001).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait poster)
- **Palette** (real *Magicicada septendecim* coloration + chalkboard):
  - `#0B0A08` — deep chitin black (substrate)
  - `#E9D9B4` — chalk / aged parchment (rules, type)
  - `#C81E1E` — Magicicada eye red (highlight, prime marker)
  - `#F2A21C` — cicada wing amber (accent, secondary type)
  - `#5B4A2E` — burnished bronze (grid, muted lines)
- **Mood:** patient, exacting, mathematical, quietly triumphant
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A single large stylized cicada rendered as a technical/scientific diagram, upper-right, orange-amber wing venation over a black body with red eyes.
  - Under it: a "proof sheet" showing horizontal tick-strips for years 1..34 — one strip per predator cycle length (2, 3, 4, 5, 6) with dense red-marked tick-hits, and one strip for the 17-year cicada with only two ticks and *no* coincidences with any predator strip.
  - A prominent numeral **17** with "PRIME" tag serving as the compositional counterweight to the cicada.
- **Motion:** a single sweeping "year cursor" advances left-to-right across the number line over ~4 seconds with easing-out; predator ticks illuminate as it passes; the 17-tick on the cicada strip lands last, ringed in red. A 5-second loop.
