# The Sky's Loudest Radio Station

**Pairing:** Aurora Borealis as Radio Broadcaster

## The hook
The same solar‑wind electrons that paint the aurora also excite a coherent cyclotron‑maser emission in the auroral cavity ~1–3 Earth radii up, radiating natural radio waves — **Auroral Kilometric Radiation** — at **100–500 kHz** with peak bursts near **10⁹ W**, briefly the brightest planetary radio source in the inner solar system (Gurnett, *JGR* 79, 4227, 1974). Earth's own ionosphere reflects the signal back into space, which is why it went undetected until spacecraft (IMP‑6, Hawkeye‑1) climbed above it.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#04081C` — polar night sky
  - `#3EE578` — 557.7 nm atomic‑oxygen green (dominant auroral line)
  - `#B95BFF` — 427.8 nm N₂⁺ First‑Negative violet
  - `#FF3D6C` — 630 nm oxygen / 670 nm nitrogen red (top of the arc)
  - `#E9E1B7` — warm bone (type, ticks, star field)
  - `#8A8F99` — cool draftsman gray (metadata, minor labels)
- **Mood:** cold, luminous, resonant.
- **Key visual elements:**
  - Full‑bleed night sky with a delicate star field.
  - A vertical stack of aurora curtains rendered as translucent broadcast ribbons — green at the base, fringing to violet with a crimson crown, drawn with the geometry of a signal envelope, not a photograph.
  - A dark low ridgeline with a single hair‑thin **radio broadcast antenna** silhouette (guy‑wired, blinking beacon) tying the "broadcaster" role to the aurora above it.
  - A precision **radio dial** across the bottom of the frame — a horizontal spectrum with tick marks at 100/200/300/400/500 kHz and a live AKR waveform trace peaking around 300 kHz.
- **Motion:** the curtains drift in a slow sinusoidal sway (Easing.inOut, ~5 s loop) while the AKR waveform scrolls right‑to‑left across the dial and a single "carrier pulse" cycles down the tallest ribbon — one primary motion (broadcast) dramatizing the hook.
