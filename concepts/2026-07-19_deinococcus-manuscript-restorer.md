# The Manuscript Restorer

**Pairing:** *Deinococcus radiodurans* as Manuscript Restorer

## The hook
A single bacterium shredded to hundreds of double-strand DNA fragments by ~5,000 Gy of ionizing radiation — roughly a thousand times the human lethal dose — patiently stitches its own genome back together within about three hours using extended synthesis-dependent strand annealing (ESDSA), overlapping the fragments and copying the missing spans into place before dividing again (Zahradka et al., *Nature* 443, 2006).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 portrait.
- **Palette (drawn from real coloration — carotenoid deinoxanthin gives the colony its salmon-pink cast, contrasted with parchment and iron-gall ink):**
  - `#12100C` — iron-gall ink (near-black warm)
  - `#EBD9B0` — aged parchment
  - `#C89B5B` — vellum highlight / gilt
  - `#E86C6C` — deinoxanthin salmon (colony coloration)
  - `#8A1F26` — deep carotenoid red
- **Mood:** reverent, patient, quietly astonishing.
- **Key visual elements:**
  - Focal object: a torn manuscript leaf ruptured by a jagged fracture, its fragments floating slightly apart on a dark restoration table.
  - Signature prop: a conservator's fine-tipped tweezers and a golden alignment guide crossing the fracture.
  - Fusing idea: the pink microbial colony blooms *along* the fracture line, its cells forming the overlap regions where ESDSA copies the missing text between fragments — the bacterium is literally re-setting the type across the tear.
- **Motion:** a spring-eased, once-only "reassembly" — fragments drift toward each other along the fracture as pink colony dots bloom in the overlap zone, sewing the two halves back into a legible page.
