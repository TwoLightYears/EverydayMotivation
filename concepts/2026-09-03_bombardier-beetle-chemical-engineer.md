# The Pulse-Jet Chemist

**Pairing:** Bombardier Beetle (*Brachinus* spp.) as Chemical Engineer

## The hook
The bombardier beetle stores hydroquinones (~10 %) and hydrogen peroxide (~25 %) in separate abdominal reservoirs; when threatened, a valve admits them to an enzyme-lined reaction chamber where catalase and peroxidase drive an exothermic reaction that ejects a boiling-water-hot (~100 °C) benzoquinone spray in discrete pulses at roughly 500 Hz — a working, valveless pulse-jet, as first quantified by Aneshansley & Eisner (*Science* 165, 1969) and later imaged in flight by synchrotron X-ray (Arndt, Moore et al., *Science* 348, 2015).

  - A small vertical temperature gauge to the left of the reactor that reads out T = 100 °C.
  - Leader-line callouts naming each part (reservoirs, catalase·peroxidase valve, reaction chamber, pulsed-jet nozzle) — the composition argues the pairing by looking exactly like an engineering datasheet drawn on an object that turns out to be alive.

- **Motion:** primary motion is a rhythmic pulse-jet — after the diagram assembles and the reservoirs fill, the mixing chamber flashes and small hot puffs eject from the nozzle in a staccato train, each puff spring-eased so it kicks out and drifts. A short pressure wave ripples back through the body on each firing, and the temperature gauge holds at 100 °C. 6-second loop.
