# The Mineral That Paints Itself

**Pairing:** Bismuth Crystal as Colourist

## The hook
The rainbow on a bismuth hopper crystal is not pigment but structural colour: as molten bismuth (Bi, Z = 83; m.p. 271.4 °C) cools it grows a bismuth-oxide (Bi₂O₃) skin only tens to hundreds of nanometres thick, and each terrace's specific thickness cancels a specific wavelength by thin-film interference — so bismuth quite literally paints itself by choosing the depth of its own oxide film.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette** (drawn from real thin-film-interference bands seen on bismuth):
  - `#0B0E13` — deep ink / substrate / void
  - `#FFC857` — bismuth gold (thin oxide, ~70 nm range)
  - `#E85D91` — bismuth magenta (~120 nm range)
  - `#2FB6C8` — bismuth teal (~180 nm range)
  - `#7A8CFF` — bismuth violet-blue (~230 nm range)
- **Mood:** exacting, jewel-like, laboratory-quiet
- **Key visual elements:**
  - The focal object is a large isometric hopper-crystal staircase — the signature stepped-square habit that bismuth forms while solidifying — occupying the upper two-thirds of the frame.
  - The colourist's signature prop: a vertical chip strip alongside the crystal, each chip showing the terrace's colour with its measured oxide thickness (nm) and dominant reflected wavelength (nm), like a Pantone/Munsell chart.
  - The composition idea that fuses them: **each stair on the crystal is itself a paint swatch**, and each chip in the strip labels the exact oxide thickness that "paints" that stair — the crystal is the palette.
- **Motion:** as the mould cools, the terraces materialise from the base upward with a gentle spring, each terrace's oxide colour easing in behind the outline; then a slow specular sheen sweeps once across the whole staircase. A ~5 s loop.
