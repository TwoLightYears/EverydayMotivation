# The Patient Archivist

**Pairing:** Peat Bog as Archivist

## The hook
In anoxic, acidic Sphagnum-dominated bogs, the moss releases *sphagnan* — a galacturonic-acid-rich polysaccharide that binds the amino groups of bacterial enzymes and immobilizes them; combined with pH 3.3–4.5 and oxygen-starved water, this keeps organic matter catalogued and intact for thousands of years. It is how Tollund Man, pulled from a Danish bog in 1950, kept his skin, hair, and the porridge of his last meal after ~2,400 years underground.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 x 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#141008` — deepest peat / substrate background
  - `#3B2A1A` — compacted peat brown
  - `#8A6236` — tannin-tea water
  - `#A8B373` — living Sphagnum green at the surface
  - `#D7B98A` — bog-preserved parchment / specimen highlight
  - `#C47B4A` — bog-iron rust accent for accession marks
- **Mood:** hushed, timeless, uncanny preservation
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A vertical stratigraphic core down the right third of the frame — accumulating peat layers from Holocene floor to living Sphagnum crown — the archivist's filing cabinet.
  - Index-card "accession tags" clipped to specific depths via thin leader lines, each with an accession number, date (cal. BP / BCE), and what the bog filed there (Tollund Man, Lindow Woman, a bronze cauldron, Mesolithic pollen).
  - A depth-to-years scale bar on the left margin; a small Sphagnum frond detail crowning the top.
- **Motion:** peat deposits upward layer-by-layer on a cubic ease, each new stratum settling into place with a soft dust; accession tags fade in from their depths after the strata are set, with a slight horizontal slide into position; the whole thing holds like a specimen page.
