# The Tunable Skin

**Pairing:** Panther Chameleon (*Furcifer pardalis*) as Nanotechnologist

## The hook
The panther chameleon does not change color by moving pigment — it tunes a photonic crystal. Its skin holds a two-layer lattice of ~130 nm guanine nanocrystals inside iridophore cells; by actively stretching that lattice, the animal shifts the wavelength it reflects across the visible spectrum, from blue (~470 nm, tight) through green and yellow to orange-red (~630 nm, loose) — demonstrated by Teyssier, Saenko, van der Marel & Milinkovitch in *Nature Communications* 6:6368 (2015).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette** (drawn from a real F. pardalis in Nosy Be color phase):
  - `#0B1024` — deep ink / substrate (relaxed / negative space)
  - `#0FB5D8` — iridophore cyan (tight lattice, short λ)
  - `#1E9E5B` — mid-spectrum emerald
  - `#F5C842` — chameleon gold
  - `#E5622A` — excited orange (loose lattice, long λ)
  - `#F0E8D2` — bone-white type / draftsman highlight
- **Mood:** exacting, iridescent, laboratory-quiet
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A single hero cross-section of chameleon skin, drawn as a scientific plate: epidermis on top, an iridophore layer visualized as an ordered lattice of small guanine dots whose spacing widens from left → right, dermis below.
  - Reflected-light rays leaving the iridophore layer as vertical columns colored by the local lattice spacing — cyan on the tight-lattice left, orange on the loose-lattice right, with the full spectrum in between.
  - Wavelength ticks (λ 470 → 630 nm) along the top; lattice-spacing ticks (d 128 → 172 nm) along the bottom.
  - Type lockup lower-left: uppercase ROLE tag, italic Playfair display headline, hook paragraph.
- **Motion:** the lattice dots grow in from left to right on a spring, and once seated the reflected-light column sweeps across the spectrum with cubic easing — one intentional pass over ~4 s that dramatizes "tune the crystal, tune the color."
