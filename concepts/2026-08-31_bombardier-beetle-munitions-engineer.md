# The Beetle That Fires Pulsed Detonations

**Pairing:** Bombardier Beetle (*Brachinus* spp.) as Munitions Engineer

## The hook
Threatened, a bombardier beetle mixes stored hydroquinones and ~25% hydrogen peroxide into a thickly-walled reaction chamber holding catalases and peroxidases; the enzymatic oxidation ejects a boiling ~100 °C quinone spray at roughly 500 pulses per second — a genuine micro-scale pulsed jet, imaged by high-speed X-ray and stroboscopy by Dean, Aneshansley, Edgerton, and Eisner in *Science* 248 (1990): 1219–1221.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0B0805` — reaction-chamber black (substrate / negative space)
  - `#D4552B` — Brachinus thorax ember orange (reservoir / body)
  - `#F2A93B` — ignition amber (reaction chamber / pulse core)
  - `#FFE39C` — flash highlight (pulse hot edge, freshest puff)
  - `#8A7E6E` — cool draftsman gray (grid, secondary type, leader lines)
- **Mood:** exacting, ballistic, technical-patent
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A patent-style cutaway of a *Brachinus* in profile, facing left, with its abdomen tip pointed right — reservoir, valve, thick-walled reaction chamber, and exit nozzle all rendered and labeled with leader lines.
  - A pulsed detonation train streaming out of the abdominal nozzle to the right: successive amber puffs, hottest at the nozzle, fading into vapor.
  - An inset chemistry callout with the exact balanced reaction (2 C₆H₄(OH)₂ + H₂O₂ → 2 C₆H₄O₂ + 2 H₂O), plus the "100 °C · 500 Hz" operating stamps.
  - A drafting-board grid with corner crop marks and a millimetre scale bar; a single strong type lockup below the frame (uppercase ROLE tag + display italic title + hook line).
- **Motion:** the reservoir contents flow through the valve into the chamber, then a rhythmic burst of pulses ejects from the nozzle — each pulse born at the nozzle with a spring, riding rightward and fading. A 5-second loop of steady pulse fire, timed to suggest the real 500 Hz pattern (visually undersampled).
