# The Hundred-Door Thermostat

**Pairing:** Sociable Weaver (*Philetairus socius*) as HVAC Engineer

## The hook

In the Kalahari, a single colony of sociable weavers assembles the largest communal nest on Earth — one shared thatched dome, 100+ breeding pairs, continuously occupied for up to a century — and the density of that straw insulation alone keeps interior chambers up to ~23 °C warmer than outside on winter desert nights (and several degrees cooler during midday peaks), with no mechanical part anywhere in the system (Bartholomew, White & Howell, *The Condor* 78, 1976, 268–273).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0F0E0C` — deep ink (night sky / substrate)
  - `#BF6A2E` — Kalahari ochre (red sand, horizon)
  - `#D9A646` — thatch gold (straw dome, interior glow)
  - `#4F7DA3` — cold-side blue (ambient-night temperature)
  - `#E8DDC5` — parchment (type / neutrals)
- **Mood:** patient, communal, architectural-meets-climatic
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A cross-sectioned sociable-weaver nest hanging from a camelthorn branch, dominating the right half of the frame; chambers inside render as a honeycomb of small warm cells, each a glowing amber.
  - A tall ambient-temperature ribbon on the left — a 24-hour thermometer bar that swings from cold blue (night, –1 °C) to hot ochre (midday, +33 °C).
  - Inside the nest, a flat line labelled "INTERIOR 22 °C" cuts across the chamber grid at a single steady value — the visual argument of the pairing.
- **Motion:** a single 5-second day/night cycle. The sun arcs across the top band; the ambient ribbon colour-shifts cold-warm-cold; the chambers inside stay the same amber glow throughout. A thermometer needle on the left sweeps; the interior readout barely moves. One easing-driven motion, calm and intentional.
