# The Honeycomb Mason

**Pairing:** Columnar Basalt as Mason

## The hook
When a thick basaltic lava flow cools uniformly, it contracts and fractures from the cooling surface inward; the crack geometry that dissipates the least elastic strain energy is a hexagonal tiling, which is why the ~40,000 columns at the Giant's Causeway in Northern Ireland are overwhelmingly six-sided, their joints meeting at 120°. That the regular hexagon minimizes the total perimeter of a plane-partition of equal areas is the *honeycomb conjecture* — proven by Thomas Hales in 1999 (*Discrete & Computational Geometry* 25, 1–22, 2001).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 x 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0F1217` — basalt shadow (background)
  - `#2B313B` — weathered column face
  - `#5C4A2A` — iron-oxide crust (intersections / tool lines)
  - `#C9A24A` — *Xanthoria* lichen gold (highlight / accents)
  - `#E8DCBE` — top-of-column bone-white (type, sun lift)
- **Mood:** austere, geometric, ancient-craft
- **Key visual elements:**
  - Centred top-down plan of a hexagonal column mosaic, stones subtly varied in tone to feel real (a few 5- and 7-sided "faults" among the hexagons).
  - The mason's signature props laid over the plan: a pair of brass dividers, a 120° angle gauge, a plumb line.
  - A single 120° joint called out in a precise technical callout with a measured arc.
  - Strong lower-left type lockup: role tag in uppercase gold, large italic serif title, single-line hook in clean sans.
- **Motion:** the mosaic grows from a central seed column — cracks propagate outward with a spring-eased stroke-reveal — then the dividers swing into place to prove the 120° angle. A 5-second settle, no loop.
