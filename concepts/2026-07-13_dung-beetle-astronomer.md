# Steers by Starlight

**Pairing:** Dung Beetle (*Scarabaeus satyrus*) as Astronomer

## The hook
On moonless nights the South African dung beetle *Scarabaeus satyrus* rolls its ball in a straight line by fixing its bearing on the diffuse band of the Milky Way itself — the first non‑human animal shown to navigate by the entire galaxy (Dacke, Baird, Byrne, Scholtz & Warrant, *Current Biology* 23, 298–300, 2013). Beetles fitted with tiny cardboard visors that occluded the sky wandered in circles; beetles under a planetarium sky containing only the galactic band walked as straight as those under the full starfield.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 x 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#070912` — night ink (deep sky / substrate)
  - `#141A34` — indigo mid‑sky
  - `#E9D6A6` — Milky Way starlight cream (galactic core)
  - `#C67553` — warm nebular dust (accent, oat‑warm)
  - `#8A6FAE` — cosmic dust purple (secondary accent)
  - `#8A8F99` — cool observatory gray (grid, secondary type)
- **Mood:** reverent, quiet, precise — a nocturnal field notebook
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A vast night sky filling the upper drafting frame, with the Milky Way drawn as a diagonal band of denser stars and warm dust from upper‑left to lower‑right.
  - A dark savanna horizon at the bottom of the sky panel; on it, a tiny silhouetted dung beetle rolling a spherical ball, walking straight along the horizon line.
  - A slender dashed bearing line rising from the beetle to a fixed point on the galactic band — the beetle's celestial "shot."
  - A right‑ascension / declination reticle over the sky panel (draftsman gray) that reinforces the "astronomer" reading.
  - Strong lower type block: uppercase ROLE tag, italic display title, single‑line hook.
- **Motion:** the Milky Way and starfield fade in first; the bearing line traces upward from the beetle with a spring easing and locks; the beetle then walks slowly across the horizon while stars twinkle at low amplitude. A calm ~5‑second loop.
