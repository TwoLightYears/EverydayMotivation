# The Beetle That Steers By The Galaxy

**Pairing:** Dung Beetle (*Scarabaeus satyrus*) as Celestial Navigator

## The hook
The nocturnal African dung beetle *Scarabaeus satyrus* uses the diffuse band of light cast by the Milky Way as a visual compass, rolling its dung ball in a straight line away from the dung pile and from competitors — the first non-human animal documented to orient by our galaxy (Dacke, Baird, Byrne, Scholtz & Warrant, *Current Biology* 23, 298–300, 2013; verified in a planetarium under a star field with and without the Milky Way band).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 x 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette** (drawn from the real scene: beetle, dung, night sky, Milky Way):
  - `#06080F` — matte-black elytra / ink void
  - `#131A2E` — nightfall navy (sky backdrop)
  - `#E8CF8E` — warm amber dung ball / Milky Way galactic core
  - `#F3E6B6` — pale starlight cream (Milky Way highlight)
  - `#5E7FB0` — cool star-field blue
  - `#8A8F99` — draftsman cool gray (type secondary, grid)
- **Mood:** cosmic, precise, nocturnal
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A circular **planetarium disc** / star chart offset asymmetrically in the upper-right of a dark field, with bearing ticks around its ring.
  - The **Milky Way band** curving diagonally across the entire panel, passing through the disc — a streak of warm cream and amber grains on a navy field.
  - At the lower-center of the panel, a horizon line with the **beetle silhouette rolling its dung ball**, head-down against the ball in the species' characteristic posture, with a thin bearing line rising from the ball toward a star in the Milky Way (the compass cue).
  - Astronomical reticle details: coordinate ticks, azimuth labels, a scale note (°, not km), a figure caption.
  - A small inset diagram showing two paths — looping (cap on) vs. straight (cap off) — the experiment's core evidence.
- **Motion:** the star field fades in and twinkles softly; the Milky Way band washes in from above; a bearing line draws itself from the beetle up to its target star while the beetle inches forward along the horizon with the ball rotating. Calm, 5-second loop.
