# The 4,800-year archivist

**Pairing:** Great Basin bristlecone pine (*Pinus longaeva*) as Archivist

## The hook

A single bristlecone pine — Methuselah, high in California's White Mountains — has laid down one distinct growth ring every year since roughly 2833 BCE, and by cross-dating overlapping bristlecone chronologies dendrochronologists have built a continuous 8,700-year archive that calibrates the radiocarbon curve used to date every organic sample on Earth.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 portrait.
- **Palette (pulled from real bristlecone coloration — sun-bleached deadwood, orange-red heartwood, resin amber, silvery bark, deep needle):**
  - `#14100E` — vault-dark near-black backing
  - `#C79A5A` — resin amber / late-wood highlights
  - `#B8A57C` — weathered sapwood cream
  - `#6C6155` — silvery strip-bark gray
  - `#384A2C` — deep needle green (accent)
  - `#E8D9B5` — bone-cream typographic ground
- **Mood:** archival, monumental, patient.
- **Key visual elements:**
  - Cross-section of an asymmetric strip-bark bristlecone trunk, filling a numbered plate; concentric growth rings dense at the pith, with a thin silver arm of exposed deadwood.
  - Radial reticle / archivist's caliper marching outward from the pith with a running year counter and centimeter tick marks.
  - Pull-out annotations that anchor specific rings to famous years witnessed (2560 BCE Great Pyramid, 776 BCE first Olympiad, 79 CE Vesuvius, 1215 Magna Carta, 1815 Tambora, 2026 CE outer bark).
  - Small `PITH · 2833 BCE` callout at dead centre.
- **Motion:** One primary motion — a slow radial paint-on from the pith outward over ~3 s (spring-eased), synchronized with a year counter ticking `2833 BCE → 2026 CE`; once the outer bark lands, the historical callouts fade in one by one.
