# The Underground Commodities Trader

**Pairing:** Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungus as Commodities Trader

## The hook

Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (phylum Glomeromycota) run a biological market with their host plants: when offered three fungal isolates in a split-root experiment, plants supplied **~3× more carbon** to the isolate delivering more phosphate, and the fungi reciprocated by moving more phosphorus to the roots that paid better — effectively quoting a variable **C : P exchange rate** and sanctioning cheating partners by withholding supply (Kiers et al., *Science* 333 (2011) 880–882).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080×1350 portrait.
- **Palette (drawn from real microscopy of trypan-blue-stained AMF arbuscules in root cortex):**
  - `#120B06` deep humus (background)
  - `#E8D7B1` root cortex cream
  - `#2E5F8E` arbuscule trypan-blue
  - `#E09344` carbon-token amber (plant sugars)
  - `#89BBD2` phosphate-token glacier blue
- **Mood:** patient, mercantile, subterranean.
- **Key visual elements:**
  - Focal object: a single arbuscule rendered as a recursive fractal "trading floor" inside a host root cell, positioned off-centre-left on the plate.
  - Role prop: a right-side "RATE BOARD" quoting three fungal isolates against the same C-unit, one tagged `FAVORED`, one tagged `SANCTIONED`.
  - Fusion idea: the arbuscule's branches ARE the market — carbon flows in along every tip, phosphate flows out the same path, both animated as discrete packets drifting in opposite directions.
- **Motion:** a continuous counter-flow of carbon (down into the arbuscule) and phosphate (up and out) along the dendrites, with a slow spring-eased reveal of the arbuscule itself and a periodic tick on the "rate board." No linear tweens — spring/cubic easing only.
