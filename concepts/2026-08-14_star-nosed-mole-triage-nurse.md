# The Mammal Triage Nurse

**Pairing:** Star-Nosed Mole (*Condylura cristata*) as ER Triage Nurse

## The hook

The star-nosed mole's snout ends in 22 fleshy tentacles studded with more than 25,000 Eimer's organs — the densest touch receptor field known in any mammal. It touches 10–13 objects per second and can identify, decide on, and swallow prey in as little as 120 milliseconds — the fastest foraging handling time ever recorded in a mammal (Catania & Remple, *Nature* 433, 2005).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait)
- **Palette:**
  - `#141519` — mole fur (near-black charcoal)
  - `#F1E7D2` — chart paper / cream ground
  - `#E7A2A6` — nose flesh pink
  - `#1F5A5E` — clinical teal (label ink, dial ring)
  - `#E3803A` — triage amber (accent for the ≤120 ms hit)
- **Mood:** precise, urgent, tactile
- **Key visual elements:** a 22-ray "nose star" centered inside a millisecond triage dial marked 0 → 120 ms; the rays double as clock ticks and as the mole's tentacles — the composition fuses "clinical chart" with "sensory fan," so the star IS the dial. A single amber ray, currently touching the outer ring, marks the successful triage.
- **Motion:** the 22 rays flick outward in sequence at ~12 touches per second (a Sunburst sweep with spring easing); each flick briefly lights the millisecond tick it lands on; every ~120 ms the amber accent-ray fires a "CONFIRM" flash — the whole loop dramatizes the mole's decision speed.
