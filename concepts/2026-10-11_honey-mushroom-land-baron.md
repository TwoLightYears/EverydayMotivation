# The Silent Baron of the Blue Mountains

**Pairing:** Honey Mushroom (*Armillaria ostoyae*) as Land Baron

## The hook

A single *Armillaria ostoyae* clone in Oregon's Malheur National Forest sprawls across **965 hectares (2,384 acres, ~3.7 mi²)** as one genetically identical mycelium — estimated **1,900–8,650 years old** and likely the largest single organism on Earth, holding its empire together through black bootlace-like rhizomorphs that creep through the soil at ~1 m/yr and quietly kill the conifers above (Ferguson et al., *Can. J. For. Res.* 33: 612–623, 2003).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait).
- **Palette (drawn from real *A. ostoyae* coloration):**
  - `#D9A24A` — honey-amber cap
  - `#F0DEB4` — pale cream gills
  - `#3A2A1A` — bootlace rhizomorph brown
  - `#4A5436` — mossy forest-floor olive
  - `#120D08` — deep-soil umbra (ground)
- **Mood:** patient, imperial, subterranean.
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A **cross-section**: a thin band of forest-floor olive splits the frame; above it a modest cluster of honey mushrooms (small, like flags on a boundary stake); below it a vast, branching **rhizomorph network** that fills the lower two-thirds — the real estate.
  - A **surveyor's cartouche** anchored at the margin, drafted like a deed: *Genet · Armillaria ostoyae · 2,384 acres · c. 2,400 yrs.*
  - A **dashed amber property line** tracing the irregular Malheur boundary around the entire sprawl.
- **Motion:** rhizomorphs germinate from a central genet and extend outward on a cubic ease; a single amber signal pulses along the longest trunk, surveying the holdings; mushroom caps and the property line resolve last.
