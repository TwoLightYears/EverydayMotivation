# The Cryptographer of the Meadow

**Pairing:** Firefly (*Photinus pyralis*) as Cryptographer

## The hook
Each *Photinus pyralis* male broadcasts a species-specific code: a ~0.5-second yellow-green flash (peak emission ≈ 560 nm) traced along a distinctive "J"-shaped ascending flight arc, repeated at ~5.5-second intervals — and predatory *Photuris* femme-fatale females decode it and mimic the answering flash of *Photinus* females to lure the males in and eat them (Lloyd, *Science* 149 (1965): 653–654; *Science* 187 (1975): 452–453).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#080B12` — night substrate (deep meadow-black)
  - `#C6E24A` — bioluminescent yellow-green (Photinus emission ≈ 560 nm)
  - `#F0F6B4` — flash bloom highlight (over-exposed pulse core)
  - `#E48A2E` — pronotum ember orange (Photinus adult body accent)
  - `#7C818C` — cool observer-log gray (grid, secondary type)
- **Mood:** nocturnal, codified, patient
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A dark field-observation panel with faint ruled grid and ember cornermarks — the cryptographer's notebook.
  - The male firefly's characteristic "J"-shaped flight arc rendered as a single glowing yellow-green pulse-trace, with the peak bloom holding the eye at the top of the arc where the firefly hangs.
  - Slim cipher-notation annotations to the right of the arc: `0.5 S PULSE · λ ≈ 560 NM · 5.5 S INTERVAL`.
  - A secondary, tiny "♀ ANSWER" bloom below-right — the response half of the code exchange.
  - Strong lower-left type block: uppercase ROLE tag, display italic title, single hook paragraph.
- **Motion:** the J-arc traces upward with spring easing as its bloom brightens progressively; at the peak, the flash holds for the real ~0.5-second pulse duration, then rests; the female "answer" bloom arrives ~2 s later on the ground line. A ~5.5-second loop matching the real male-flash interval.
