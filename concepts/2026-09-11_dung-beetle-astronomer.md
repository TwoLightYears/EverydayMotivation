# The Astronomer with Six Legs

**Pairing:** Dung Beetle (*Scarabaeus satyrus*) as Astronomer

## The hook
On moonless African nights the ball-rolling beetle *Scarabaeus satyrus* keeps a perfectly straight bearing away from the dung pile by orienting to the polarised light gradient of the Milky Way's disc — the first insect ever shown to navigate by the light of our galaxy (Dacke et al., *Current Biology* 23:298–300, 2013).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 x 1350 (portrait poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#080B18` — night sky ink (substrate / negative space)
  - `#F2E1B8` — starlight cream (stars, primary type)
  - `#7C6EE0` — Milky Way violet (galactic band core)
  - `#B0821F` — beetle carapace bronze (focal object, oat-flake-scale accent)
  - `#5A6B8C` — dust-lane cool grey (secondary type, plotting grid)
- **Mood:** hushed, observatory-precise, cosmological
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A luminous Milky Way band arcing across the upper two-thirds of the composition, rendered as a soft gradient with visible dust-lane articulation.
  - A star-chart bearing wheel (compass rose graduated in polarisation degrees) overlaid discreetly on the sky — the beetle's celestial instrument.
  - A silhouetted *Scarabaeus satyrus* rolling its ball along a razor-straight dashed trajectory on the horizon, its heading vector snapping to the galactic pole.
  - Strong lower-left type block: uppercase ROLE tag, large italic Playfair display title, single-line hook in Inter.
- **Motion:** the trajectory dashes in from the origin along a spring easing while the compass rose rotates once to lock onto the galactic bearing, then a slow pulse migrates along the Milky Way's dust lane. 5-second loop.
