# The Frog Who Practices Cryonics

**Pairing:** Alaskan Wood Frog (*Rana sylvatica*) as a Cryonicist

## The hook

Every winter, Alaskan wood frogs stop their own heart, silence their brain, and let ice replace **up to 65–70% of their body water** — cells surviving because the frog floods them with a self-generated glucose cryoprotectant that spikes roughly **100-fold above summer baseline** just as freezing begins (Larson, Middle et al., *J. Exp. Biol.* 217, 2014, 2193–2200; Storey & Storey, *J. Comp. Physiol. B* 155, 1984).

## Visual brief

**Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 portrait.

**Palette** (drawn from the animal and its habitat — snowpack, borealis-lit ice, glucose amber, and the frog's dorsal russet):
- `#0B1522` frozen night — background
- `#7FA8BE` frost cyan — crystals, monitor lines
- `#C8DDE8` ice pale — highlights, type
- `#E8B655` glucose amber — single accent (baseline trace, key figure)
- `#6B4E3D` wood-frog russet — the animal

**Mood:** hushed, clinical, quietly mythic.

**Key visual elements:**
- **Focal object.** A stylised wood frog in resting crouch, held mid-block inside a rectangular slab of ice — the ice is drawn with radiating fracture geometry and hexagonal snowflake motifs at the four corners.
- **Role prop.** A single clinical monitor trace running under the specimen: heart-rate decelerating from beat to flatline, with a small tick where beating resumes at the end of the loop.
- **Fusion idea.** The frog is both patient and physician — the ice block is its own cryogenic capsule, annotated in the language of a cryobiology plate (temperature, glucose concentration, ice fraction) via margin gutters.

**Motion:** the primary motion is *frost propagation* — hexagonal ice crystals grow outward from the frog's silhouette along easing curves, while a single glucose-amber pulse threads through the frog's body before the crystals overtake it. The heart trace decays synchronously with the crystal growth; a single beat rekindles at the loop's tail.
