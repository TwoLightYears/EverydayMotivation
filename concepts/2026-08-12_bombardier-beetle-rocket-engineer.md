# The Beetle Who Beat Us to the Pulse-Jet

**Pairing:** Bombardier Beetle (*Brachinus* spp.) as Rocket Engineer

## The hook
When a bombardier beetle is threatened it mixes hydroquinones and ~25% hydrogen peroxide from two abdominal reservoirs into a reaction chamber lined with catalase and peroxidase; the runaway exothermic reaction reaches ~100 °C and is expelled as a boiling defensive spray in a train of ~500 discrete pulses per second — a natural pulse-jet, timed by high-speed thermography, that has been studied to inform pulsed-combustion propulsion (Aneshansley et al., *Science* 165, 1969; Dean et al., *Science* 248, 1990; Beheshti & McIntosh, *Bioinspir. Biomim.* 2, 2007).

  - `#8A8F99` — cool draftsman gray (grid, secondary type)
- **Mood:** exacting, mechanical, quietly explosive
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A blueprint-style dorsal cutaway of the beetle: orange pronotum, dark elytra, and an exposed abdomen tip with two labeled reservoirs (**HYDROQUINONES**, **H₂O₂**) feeding into a small reaction chamber lined with catalase/peroxidase, venting through a rear turret.
  - Pulsed emission at the turret rendered as a staged column of concentric amber puffs — the ~500 Hz pulse train made visible.
  - Type block below the frame: uppercase role tag, italic Playfair title, single-line hook. Left-aligned to the margin grid; asymmetric weight.
- **Motion:** the schematic assembles head-to-tail, reservoir gauges tick to full, the chamber ignites, and puffs strobe outward from the turret on a spring cadence — a calm 5-second loop.
