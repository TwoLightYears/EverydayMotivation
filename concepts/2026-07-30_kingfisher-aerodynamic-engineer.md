# The Bird Behind the Bullet Train

**Pairing:** Common Kingfisher (*Alcedo atthis*) as Aerodynamic Engineer

## The hook
Between 1990 and 1997, JR‑West's Eiji Nakatsu reshaped the Shinkansen 500's 15‑metre nose after the common kingfisher's beak — the compound curve that lets the bird pierce water without a visible splash by smoothly ramping the pressure change across the air–water interface. The redesign eliminated the tunnel‑boom pressure wave that had plagued the 300‑series, cut power consumption by ~15%, and raised the top service speed by ~10% (from 270 to 300 km/h).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette** (all drawn from real *Alcedo atthis* plumage & JR‑West livery):
  - `#0B1424` — deep water / ink (substrate & negative space)
  - `#1F3B70` — kingfisher cobalt back
  - `#3FBEC9` — kingfisher turquoise rump / throat
  - `#E86A2C` — kingfisher rust chest & belly
  - `#F0EAD6` — pale throat / paper highlight
  - `#8A8F99` — cool draftsman gray (grid, secondary type, dimension lines)
- **Mood:** precise, hushed, blueprint‑meets‑ornithology
- **Key visual elements:**
  - Upper half: a stylised kingfisher head in profile, beak leading downward at ~30°, rendered as flat SVG shapes with clean colour blocks.
  - The **outer beak curve** is the hero — it continues off the beak tip, sweeps through the middle third, and *becomes* the Shinkansen 500's nose profile in the lower half, drawn in ghosted line as an engineering elevation.
  - Blueprint annotations: dashed central axis, dimension marks on the shared curve, small labels ("BEAK PROFILE", "NOSE PROFILE / 15 M"), and corner crop marks.
  - Type block in the lower quarter: uppercase role tag, italic display headline, single hook line.
- **Motion:** the shared profile curve traces itself first (stroke‑dashoffset, spring easing) — bird head fills above, train silhouette fills below in sequence — title & hook spring in last. 5‑second loop.
