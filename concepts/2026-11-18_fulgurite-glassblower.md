# Petrified Lightning

**Pairing:** Fulgurite as Glassblower

## The hook
When lightning strikes sand, the current channel reaches roughly 30,000 K — hotter than the Sun's surface — and in a few milliseconds it vaporises a thread of silica and fuses the surrounding grains into a hollow, branching tube of amorphous SiO₂ (lechatelierite) that preserves the exact path the current took through the ground. The ground does the glassblowing; the bolt is only the breath.

## Visual brief
- **Dimensions:** 1080×1350 portrait.
- **Palette (drawn from real fulgurite coloration + storm sky):**
  - `#070A14` — deep storm-night ink (background)
  - `#C9A26A` — warm desert-sand ochre
  - `#F2E4C6` — pale cream (glass highlight, fulgurite interior)
  - `#A8B9FF` — electric blue-white lightning
  - `#FFB36A` — ember amber (molten afterglow where strike meets sand)
- **Mood:** Electric, geological, forged-in-a-flash.
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A specimen-plate cross-section split by a horizon line: stormy night sky above, sand strata below.
  - A single lightning bolt branches down from the top, converging on one impact point on the horizon.
  - Mirror-inverted below the horizon: a hollow, branching fulgurite tube grows downward from that same impact point through the sand — the lightning's "negative" preserved as glass.
  - Scientific labels (lightning channel ≈ 30,000 K; silica melting point 1,713 °C; depth scale) anchor the composition to real physics.
- **Motion:** Lightning dashes from sky to horizon with a sharp easing (strike), a brief ember flash at the impact point, then the fulgurite tube grows downward along a traced stroke — the glass remembering the current path. Title springs in after the strike settles; hook fades in last. A soft amber ember pulses at the strike point for the rest of the loop.
