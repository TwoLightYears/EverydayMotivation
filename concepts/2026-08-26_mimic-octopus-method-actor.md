# The Eight-Armed Impersonator

**Pairing:** Mimic Octopus (*Thaumoctopus mimicus*) as Method Actor

## The hook
The mimic octopus is one of the very few animals shown to impersonate multiple defended species by combining shape, coloration, *and* behaviour — documented mimicking at least 15 models (banded sole, lion-fish, banded sea-snake among them) by re-arranging its arms, shifting its stripe pattern, and adopting the target's swim gait; described formally as dynamic behavioural mimicry by Norman, Finn & Tregenza, *Proc. R. Soc. B* 268 (2001): 1755–1758.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:** drawn from the mimic octopus's real dark-brown-on-cream banding and its warning-red "lion-fish" persona.
  - `#100A08` — deep stage-black (backdrop / negative space)
  - `#F1E4C7` — warm cream (light bands, spotlight, paper)
  - `#3A2416` — dark chocolate umber (dark bands, ink type)
  - `#C9432B` — lion-fish crimson (accent, one persona)
  - `#8FA9AE` — pale seafoam grey (sea-snake / secondary role)
- **Mood:** theatrical, hushed, playbill-precise
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A single, centrally-placed silhouette of the octopus caught mid-transformation — one arm still curled octopus-like, the others already fanning outward as lion-fish spines. A soft top-down spotlight isolates it against the black stage.
  - A four-panel "cast list" strip running under the hero, each cell a stylised silhouette of one impersonated species (octopus base, lion-fish, banded sole, banded sea-snake) with the model name set small.
  - Playbill-style type lockup lower-left: uppercase ROLE tag, italic display headline, then the hook in a single measured paragraph.
- **Motion:** one primary morph — arm postures and body stripes ease through the four personas in sequence (octopus → lion-fish → sole → sea-snake → back), driven by a spring, held on each pose for ~1 s. A subtle spotlight breathes with the transformation.
