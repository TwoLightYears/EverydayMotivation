# Rolls by starlight

**Pairing:** African Dung Beetle (*Scarabaeus satyrus*) as Astronomer

## The hook

On moonless nights, *Scarabaeus satyrus* keeps its dung ball travelling in a dead-straight line by orienting to the diffuse band of light cast by the Milky Way — the first known invertebrate to use the galaxy itself for navigation (Dacke et al., *Current Biology*, 2013; confirmed in a planetarium when beetles given only a projected Milky Way still rolled straight, but wandered under a starless sky).

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, Instagram 4:5)
- **Palette (drawn from the real coloration of a Kalahari night + the beetle):**
  - `#070812` — deep space ink
  - `#141A2E` — midnight sky panel
  - `#F5EBCF` — galactic-band cream (the Milky Way's actual visual tone)
  - `#C99659` — galactic dust-lane amber / beetle highlight
  - `#D4A24C` — chart accent (echo of beetle elytra sheen)
  - `#7B8094` — chart gridlines, labels
- **Mood:** cosmic, patient, instrumental — a sky chart that happens to be solved by a beetle.
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A plate-style star chart filling the hero area; the Milky Way arcs diagonally across it as a soft cream band with a darker dust lane.
  - A small chart-style icon of the beetle at the bottom of the plate, with a **dead-straight dashed trajectory** leaving it — the composition idea that fuses the pair.
  - A faint sight-line / bearing from the galactic band down to the beetle's position, marked with a bearing annotation ("θ = 0.0°").
  - Astronomical chart furniture: RA/Dec grid, declination labels, scale in degrees, a FIG. caption.
- **Motion:** the galactic band and star field fade in first, then a single bearing-ray resolves from galaxy to beetle, and the straight trajectory draws itself out — a calm, easing-driven reveal that dramatizes "the beetle fixes a bearing, then walks it."
