# The Sky's Glassblower

**Pairing:** Lightning as Glassblower

## The hook
A cloud-to-ground lightning stroke heats the air in its channel to roughly 30,000 K — about five times the Sun's photospheric temperature — and when the bolt strikes silica-rich sand it instantly fuses the grains along its path into a hollow, branching tube of amorphous silica glass (lechatelierite) called a fulgurite: a frozen negative cast of the bolt's descent through the ground.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0A0F1C` — deep ink (night sky / substrate)
  - `#BDE4FF` — bolt-ice blue (lightning plasma)
  - `#FF8EC3` — hot plasma pink (edge of the channel)
  - `#C9985A` — sand (horizon / soil layer)
  - `#F4D06F` — fulgurite amber (fused silica glass, glowing from within)
- **Mood:** elemental, forensic, hushed-after-the-strike
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A single vertical axis bisecting the frame: a jagged white-hot lightning bolt descending from the top, striking a thin sand horizon, and continuing beneath it as an amber-glowing fulgurite — the glass negative-cast of the same jagged path.
  - A thin horizon band and depth scale on the left mark the ground-plane cross-section.
  - Leader-line annotations label *LIGHTNING CHANNEL · ≈ 30,000 K* above and *FULGURITE · FUSED SiO₂* below.
  - Lower-left type block: ROLE tag, Playfair italic headline, single-paragraph hook.
- **Motion:** the bolt flash-materialises in one spring frame; the sand horizon briefly flares pink-white; then the fulgurite grows downward segment-by-segment on a cubic-ease, as if cooling into shape, with branches snapping into place after the main shaft reaches them. A 4-second loop.
