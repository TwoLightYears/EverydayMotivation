# The Falcon's Inlet Cone

**Pairing:** Peregrine Falcon (*Falco peregrinus*) as Aeronautical Engineer

## The hook
In a hunting stoop the peregrine sustains speeds above 320 km/h — the fastest of any animal, measured at 389 km/h in a controlled dive by Franklin's 2005 skydive study — and small pointed bony tubercles inside each naris break the incoming airflow into a slower rotational stream, preventing the ram-air pressure at those speeds from over-inflating the lung.

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0B111C` — deep night sky (background)
  - `#4B5B72` — peregrine slate (dorsal plumage / silhouette)
  - `#E7D6A8` — falcon cream (ventral / warm accent)
  - `#B87455` — rufous barring (rust callout / annotation accent)
  - `#8A96A8` — draftsman gray (grid, secondary type)
- **Mood:** taut, precise, blueprint-meets-raptor
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A single silhouetted peregrine in a full stoop, diving diagonally down the frame — wings tucked into a compact teardrop, tail streaming.
  - Thin engineering-drawing airflow curves parting around the head and body, converging at an inset callout of the nostril showing the bony tubercle that meters the airflow.
  - A speed vector annotation reading `V ≈ 320 KM/H` along the flight line, plus altitude ticks and stoop-angle notation — the aeronautical engineer's shorthand.
  - Faint blueprint grid backdrop; strong type block below with role tag, italic display title, and a single-line hook.
- **Motion:** the falcon holds still while the airflow curves streak past on a spring easing, the speed vector counts up from 0 to 320, and the nostril callout draws its own leader line last — a taut 5-second loop.
