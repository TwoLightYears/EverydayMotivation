# The clear-blooded clinician

**Pairing:** Antarctic Icefish as Hematologist

## The hook
The crocodile icefish (family *Channichthyidae*) is the only vertebrate on Earth without hemoglobin — its blood is translucent — and in −1.9 °C Southern Ocean water it keeps that colorless blood from freezing by secreting antifreeze glycoproteins (AFGPs) that bind irreversibly to the exposed faces of nascent ice crystals and halt their lattice growth. To offset the ~90% drop in oxygen-carrying capacity, its heart is 2–3× the mass of a comparable red-blooded notothenioid.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 portrait.
- **Palette:** `#06111E` (abyssal navy), `#0F2B4A` (Southern Ocean deep), `#88B9D8` (ice-crystal cold blue), `#E9F2FA` (translucent flesh / plasma), `#F7B9BA` (icefish gill pink).
- **Mood:** clinical, silent, polar.
- **Key visual elements:** a tall upright blood-draw vial as the vertical axis of the composition — half-filled with the icefish suspended inside it like a cryo-specimen, its gills the only warm note; an orthogonal clinical chart printed to the left of the vial (patient = *Chaenocephalus aceratus*, Hb = 0.0 g/dL, AFGP-8 = positive, cardiac mass index 220%); feathered ice-crystal dendrites growing outward from the vial walls, their tips stopped mid-air by small ribbon-glyphs representing bound AFGP molecules.
- **Motion:** one primary easing-driven motion — the ice-crystal lattice extends out from the vial edges along branched paths with a spring deceleration, then the growth freezes mid-reach as the AFGP ribbons snap into place at each tip; the fish itself drifts a few pixels on a slow sinusoid.
