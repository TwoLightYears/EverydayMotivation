# The Mineral Cathedral Builder

**Pairing:** Bismuth crystal as Cathedral Architect

## The hook
When bismuth is cooled slowly from its melt, its corners and edges crystallize faster than its faces — a kinetic instability that produces terraced *hopper crystals* built as concentric stepped pyramids, essentially self-assembling ziggurat cathedrals; their signature rainbow skin is thin-film interference on a native bismuth-oxide (Bi₂O₃) layer roughly 30–300 nm thick, which is why the colors shift with the film's thickness across the surface.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0B0D18` — deep charcoal (background / negative space)
  - `#7A3DB0` — bismuth purple (dominant terrace tint)
  - `#E64EA6` — magenta iridescence
  - `#2FC5D9` — cyan iridescence
  - `#F0C24A` — warm gold highlight
  - `#C7D0DE` — cool graphite gray (draftsman type, dimension lines)
- **Mood:** iridescent, monumental, architectural-mystic
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A large isometric hopper crystal — nested, terraced parallelogram-plan pyramid — occupying the upper composition, drawn as an architect would render a stepped cathedral in axonometric.
  - A thin-film-interference gradient sweeping across the terraces (purple → magenta → cyan → gold) — the mineral's actual iridescence.
  - Fine dimension lines and a leader annotation calling out the oxide-film thickness range.
  - Lower-left type lockup: uppercase ROLE tag, large italic serif title, single-line hook.
- **Motion:** a slow iridescent shimmer sweeps left-to-right across the crystal (dramatizes the thin-film interference), while the terraces materialize outward from the apex in a spring cascade. Gentle 5-second loop.
