# The 389 km/h Flight Instructor

**Pairing:** Peregrine Falcon (*Falco peregrinus*) as Fighter Pilot

## The hook
In a hunting stoop the peregrine falcon accelerates past 389 km/h (242 mph — measured on a trained bird by skydiver Ken Franklin, 2005), making it the fastest animal on Earth. To breathe at that speed, small bony cones inside its nostrils — nasal tubercles — diffuse the incoming supersonic-adjacent airstream, the same principle used by the inlet cones of a jet engine to keep high-speed air from collapsing the compressor behind it.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 x 1350 (portrait, social poster)
- **Palette:**
  - `#0F1520` — deep stratosphere ink (negative space, sky above)
  - `#334760` — falcon-back slate blue-gray (silhouette body)
  - `#E9CDA6` — dawn buff (falcon breast, horizon band)
  - `#D96A1E` — cere / talon orange (accent, callouts, reticle)
  - `#F0EBE1` — HUD cream (type, ticks, thin schematic lines)
- **Mood:** cold, precise, quietly ferocious — an F-16 checklist stapled to a naturalist's field notebook
- **Key visual elements:**
  - A stylised peregrine locked into its stoop tear-drop, angled diagonally across the frame from upper-left to lower-right, wings clamped close.
  - Thin HUD scaffolding around it — altitude ticks up the left edge, an airspeed strip in the upper-right reading "389 KM/H · MACH 0.32", angle-of-attack pip and target reticle over the silhouette.
  - A single callout leader-lined into the falcon's nostril labelled "NASAL TUBERCLE · AIRFLOW DIFFUSER" — the one detail the poster is really about.
  - Bottom lockup: uppercase ROLE tag, large italic display line, and the one-sentence hook.
- **Motion:** the falcon enters from the top and drops into its stoop position on a stiff spring; behind it, the airflow streamlines etch in like ink on a technical drawing, then the speed readout counts up from 0 to 389 on an ease-out. A single 5-second beat, held.
