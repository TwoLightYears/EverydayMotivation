# The Prime-Number Cipher

**Pairing:** Periodical Cicada (*Magicicada septendecim*) as Cryptographer

## The hook
*Magicicada* broods emerge only on 13- or 17-year cycles — both prime — so
the interval between mass emergences shares no common factor with any
predator that reproduces on a 2-, 3-, 4-, 5-, or 6-year rhythm. Modelling by
Goles, Schulz & Markus (*Complexity*, 2001) shows that a prime cycle is the
uniquely optimal way to desynchronise from every shorter-period hunter — a
number-theoretic key the cicadas encode into their life history.

## Visual brief

- **Dimensions:** 1080 × 1350 (portrait)
- **Palette** (from *Magicicada*'s real coloration on a naturalist's plate):
  - `#F1EAD6` — aged parchment (background)
  - `#1A140E` — cicada-body ink (typography + silhouette)
  - `#D8351B` — Magicicada eye red (emergence accent)
  - `#E8912A` — wing-vein amber (secondary accent)
  - `#7A6A55` — muted umber (metadata, gridlines)
- **Mood:** studious, patient, quietly triumphant.
- **Key visual elements:**
  - Focal object — a stylised dorsal *Magicicada* rendered in ink, wings
    outspread, the specimen of an old naturalist's plate.
  - Role prop — a horizontal 17-year timeline as a cryptographer's tape:
    seventeen year-ticks, sixteen silent, the seventeenth ignited.
  - Fusing composition — a bold numeral **17** set as a serif capital in
    negative space beside the cicada, doubling as brood-label and
    cipher-key.
- **Motion:** the timeline advances year-by-year with a spring-eased tick
  reveal; at year 17 the terminal tick flashes red and the cicada's red
  eye catches the same light — a single, calm crescendo.
